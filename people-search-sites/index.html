People Search Sites: What They Know About You

People-search sites make it easier to find information about individuals online. Learn what these sites are, where their information can come from, how to check your listings, and what you can do to reduce your exposure.

What are people-search sites?

People-search sites are online services that organize information about individuals and make it searchable by names, phone numbers, email addresses, locations or other identifying details.

Depending on the service and the information available, a listing may contain details such as a person's name, approximate location, previous locations, phone numbers, relatives, professional information or links to other public records.

The amount and type of information varies significantly between websites and between individuals.

Where do people-search sites get information?

Information can be collected or assembled from a variety of sources. These may include public records, directories, websites, marketing databases and other publicly or commercially available information.

Some services combine information from multiple sources to create a single profile. This can make information that is scattered across different places easier to find.

Important: A listing on a people-search site does not necessarily mean that your accounts have been hacked. Information may have been collected from public or commercially available sources.

What information might appear?

Depending on the website and available records, people-search profiles may include:

  • Names and name variations
  • Current or previous locations
  • Phone numbers
  • Email addresses
  • Possible relatives or household connections
  • Professional or business information
  • Public-record information
  • Links to related online profiles or records

Not every profile contains all of these details, and information can be outdated or incorrectly matched.

How to check if you appear on people-search sites

1. Search your full name

Start with your name in a search engine. Try quotation marks around your full name and include a city, region or other non-sensitive identifying detail when appropriate.

2. Search your phone number

Search your phone number using common formatting variations. This can help identify directories or pages where the number is publicly associated with you.

3. Search your email address

Search exact email addresses you have used publicly. Older email addresses can sometimes remain connected to abandoned profiles or websites.

4. Check the relevant profiles

If you find a people-search listing, review what information is displayed and determine whether it actually belongs to you before taking action.

People-search sites and data brokers

People-search sites and data brokers overlap, but the terms are not always interchangeable. A data broker generally collects and aggregates information for purposes such as marketing, analytics, identity-related services or other commercial uses.

People-search services commonly present information in a form that allows individuals to search for other people.

Understanding both categories is useful when trying to reduce your overall online exposure.

How to reduce your exposure

The exact removal process depends on the website. Some services provide an opt-out process that allows a person to request removal of a listing.

Common steps include:

  1. Identify the website and locate its opt-out instructions.
  2. Find the profile or listing that belongs to you.
  3. Follow the site's identity or verification process if required.
  4. Submit the removal request.
  5. Check again later to confirm whether the information was removed.

Removal is not always permanent. Information can reappear when a service receives new data or creates another profile.

Manual removal vs. removal services

Manual removal can work when you only have a small number of listings to manage. The process can become more time-consuming when information appears across many websites.

Frequently asked questions

Can I remove myself from people-search sites?

Many people-search websites provide an opt-out or removal process, but the requirements differ by site and removal may not be permanent.

Why does my information appear if I never created an account?

A profile may be assembled from public records, directories, websites or other commercially available information. You do not necessarily need to create an account with the people-search site itself.

Does finding my information mean I was hacked?

Not necessarily. Publicly available or commercially collected information can appear on people-search services without a security breach involving your accounts.

Will removing one listing remove everything about me online?

No. Removing a single listing addresses that particular source. Other websites, directories, data brokers or public records may still contain information about you.
