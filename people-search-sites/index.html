People Search Sites: What They Show and How to Remove Yourself (2026)

Typing your own name into a search engine and finding your home address, phone number and relatives on a site you have never used is a horrible surprise. You did nothing wrong, and you can do something about it. This guide explains what people-search sites are, how they get your details, whether they are legal and how to remove yourself for free.

By Siddharth Shankar Updated: November 10, 2026 Jump to: how to remove yourself
TL;DR: people-search sites

People-search sites gather public records and commercial data into profiles that anyone can look up by name, phone number or address. They are generally legal in the US, often inaccurate, and almost all let you opt out for free. Search your name, phone and email to find your listings, opt out of the biggest sites first, and recheck every three months because profiles can return. If you appear on many sites, compare data removal services by country.

What are people-search sites?

People-search sites are websites that organize information about individuals and make it searchable. You type in a name, a phone number, an email or an address, and the site shows a profile that may include where someone lives, who they live with and how to contact them.

Well-known examples include Whitepages, Spokeo, TruePeopleSearch, FastPeopleSearch, BeenVerified and TruthFinder. Many show a free summary and charge for a full report or a subscription, which is why a search often shows just enough to make you worry.

Where do people-search sites get your information?

Most profiles are assembled automatically from records that were each available somewhere:

  • property and land records, which link your name to your address
  • voter files, court records and other public records
  • old phone books and directory listings
  • marketing data from apps, loyalty schemes and online sign-ups
  • other data brokers, which sell data to each other

Because the same sources feed many sites, the same details tend to appear on several of them at once. Our guide to why people-search sites have your address traces this in more detail.

Important: a listing on a people-search site does not mean your accounts have been hacked. The information usually comes from public or commercial sources, not from your passwords or devices.

What information might appear?

Depending on the site and the records available, a profile may include:

  • your name, nicknames and former names
  • current and previous addresses
  • phone numbers and email addresses
  • your age or year of birth
  • possible relatives and people you have lived with
  • employers and business links
  • court, property or other public-record details
  • links to social profiles

Not every profile contains all of these, and much of it can be outdated or mixed up with someone else. Seeing relatives or an old address you left years ago is common; our guide on why old information and relatives keep appearing explains why.

How to check if you appear on people-search sites

1. Search your full name

Put your name in quotation marks and add your city, then a past city. Check the first two pages of results and note every people-search site that shows a profile that is really you.

2. Search your phone number

Try it with and without dashes. Reverse phone lookups often lead straight to a profile with your address. See whether someone can find your address from your phone number, and our phone number exposure check.

3. Search your email address

Include old addresses. They can stay linked to profiles long after you stop using them. Our email exposure check covers this.

4. Confirm each profile is really you

Common names bring up strangers. Match at least two details, such as a relative's name and a past address, before you act on a listing.

Are people-search sites legal?

In the US, generally yes. Much of what they publish comes from records that are public by law, and no federal law bans compiling and selling them. The law does set limits, though.

  • They cannot be used for big decisions. Reports used to decide on a job, a home, credit or insurance fall under the Fair Credit Reporting Act. That is why most people-search sites say their reports may not be used for those purposes. In 2012 the FTC announced that Spokeo would pay $800,000 to settle charges that it marketed profiles to recruiters without following that law.
  • State laws give you rights. California residents can delete their data from every registered broker with one free DROP request, and several other states let you ask companies to delete your data.
  • Some people get extra protection. New Jersey's Daniel's Law lets judges, police officers and other covered people order sites to take down their home address.

Outside the US, people-search sites are far less common, and laws such as the UK and EU GDPR give you a right to ask for your data to be erased.

Are people-search sites accurate?

Often, not very. Profiles are matched automatically by name, age and location, which leads to predictable mistakes: two people with the same name merged into one profile, phone numbers you gave up years ago, "relatives" guessed from shared addresses and estimated ages. Many sites say in their terms that they do not guarantee accuracy.

That cuts both ways. Never treat a report about someone else as fact, and do not assume a wrong report about you is harmless, because a stranger may still act on it.

People-search sites, data brokers and background checks

The terms overlap, but they are not the same:

  • People-search sites let anyone look up a person and see a profile.
  • Data brokers collect and trade data, often for marketing or risk checks, and many never show it to the public.
  • Background check companies that screen tenants or employees are consumer reporting agencies, so the FCRA gives you the right to see and dispute what they report. See your tenant screening rights and employer background check rights.

Most people need to deal with all three, which is why our data broker removal guide covers people-search sites and brokers together.

How to remove yourself from people-search sites

The good news: almost every people-search site lets you opt out for free. The routine is the same everywhere.

  1. Use a separate email address. Most sites send a confirmation link, and an email alias keeps your main inbox off yet another list.
  2. Start with the biggest sites. Whitepages, Spokeo, TruePeopleSearch, FastPeopleSearch and the PeopleConnect sites are seen most often.
  3. Copy your exact profile URL. Many forms ask for it, so copy it while the listing is open.
  4. Submit the form and click the confirmation link. Give only what the form needs.
  5. Log each request with the site, URL and date.
  6. Recheck every three months and resubmit if a profile returns.

One request can sometimes cover several sites. A single PeopleConnect suppression hides your report from name searches on TruthFinder, Instant Checkmate, Intelius and US Search. For templates and the order to work in, follow our complete opt-out routine.

Can you completely remove yourself from people-search sites?

Not permanently, in most cases. Sites refresh their data from public records and from each other, so a move, a new phone number or a new record can create a fresh profile weeks or months after you opted out. That is not a failure on your part. It is why removal works best as a routine, with a quick recheck every three months.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find out which people-search sites have my information?

Search your full name with your city, then your phone number and email, and note every people-search site in the first two pages of results. Confirm each profile is really you by matching two details.

Is it free to remove yourself from people-search sites?

Yes. Almost every site offers a free opt-out. You never have to pay a site to remove your own profile, and you should be wary of any that asks you to.

How long does it take to remove yourself?

Most big sites process opt-outs within a few minutes to about a week. Search engines can keep showing an old result for a while after the profile is gone.

Why did my profile come back after I opted out?

People-search sites refresh their data regularly, and a new address, phone number or record can create a new profile. Recheck every three months and resubmit when needed.

Are people-search sites legal?

In the US, generally yes, because much of their data comes from public records. Their reports may not be used for decisions about jobs, housing, credit or insurance, and state laws such as California's give you the right to have your data deleted.
