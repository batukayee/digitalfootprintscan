Digital Footprint Check: How to See What the Internet Knows About You

Your digital footprint is everything about you that can be found online: search results, social profiles, old accounts, your email and phone number, public records and the profiles data brokers build about you. Checking it can feel daunting, but you only need a free half hour and this guide. We will go through it together, one step at a time.

By Siddharth Shankar Updated: November 10, 2026 Time needed: about 30 minutes Cost: free

You do not need to be a cybersecurity expert to check your digital footprint. A calm, systematic search shows you what strangers, employers and scammers can find, and where to start fixing it.

TL;DR: how to check your digital footprint

Open a private browser window and search your name in quotation marks, then your emails, phone numbers and old usernames. Run a reverse image search on your profile photos, check whether your email appears in data breaches, look yourself up on the big people-search sites and view your social profiles while logged out. Write down what you find, fix the riskiest items first and repeat every three months.

What is a digital footprint?

A digital footprint is the information and activity connected to you online. It comes in two kinds:

  • Active footprint: what you share on purpose, such as social posts, reviews, comments and profiles.
  • Passive footprint: what is collected or published about you without you posting it, such as people-search profiles, data broker records, breach data and public records.

The passive part is usually the bigger surprise, because you never see it being made. Our guide to active and passive digital footprints has real examples of each.

What can be part of your digital footprint?

  • your full name and its variations
  • email addresses and phone numbers
  • your home address and past addresses
  • social media profiles, photos and old posts
  • usernames and forgotten accounts
  • forum posts, reviews and comments
  • public records and directory listings
  • people-search and data broker profiles
  • your details in past data breaches

How to check your digital footprint in 8 steps

Before you start, open a private or incognito window so your results are not shaped by your own search history, and keep a notes file or spreadsheet open to record what you find.

  1. Search your name.

    Search your full name in quotation marks, then add your city, a past city, your employer or a former name. Look through at least the first two pages. Try Bing as well as Google, because results differ.

  2. Search your email addresses.

    Search each address in quotation marks, including old ones. Then check whether it appears in known data breaches. Our email exposure check explains what the results mean.

  3. Search your phone numbers.

    Try different formats, with and without spaces or dashes. A phone number often leads to a people-search profile showing your address. See our phone number exposure check.

  4. Search your usernames.

    Reused usernames link accounts together, including ones you forgot. See what people can find from your username.

  5. Search for pictures of yourself.

    Upload your main profile photos to a reverse image search such as Google Lens, Bing Visual Search or TinEye. This shows where the same photo appears elsewhere, including on sites you did not post to.

  6. Check people-search sites.

    Look yourself up on Whitepages, Spokeo, TruePeopleSearch and FastPeopleSearch. These sites often show your address, age and relatives. Learn how people-search sites work.

  7. View your social profiles as a stranger.

    Log out, or use the view-as-public option where a network offers one, and look at what anyone can see: profile details, old posts, tagged photos and friend lists.

  8. Set up ongoing alerts.

    Turn on Google's free Results about you tool so you are told when your phone, address or email appears in search.

Search tricks that find more

A few simple search operators make your check far more thorough. They work in Google and mostly in Bing too.

Type this What it does
"Jane Doe"Finds your exact name, not the words scattered on a page
"Jane Doe" DenverNarrows results to pages that also mention your city
"Jane Doe" -actressRemoves results about someone else with your name
"Jane Doe" site:linkedin.comShows only results from one website
"jane.doe@example.com"Finds pages that show your exact email address
"555-123-4567" OR "555 123 4567"Catches your phone number in either format

How to judge what you find

Not everything you find needs fixing. Sort each item into one of three groups, so you spend your energy where it matters most:

Risk Examples What to do
HighHome address with your name, ID numbers, leaked passwords, private photosAct this week: opt out, request removal, change passwords
MediumPhone number, email, relatives' names, old social profilesOpt out or tighten privacy settings over the next month
LowA professional profile, articles you chose to publishKeep, but check they show only what you want

Check for personal information exposed online

Your name, contact details and other identifying information can spread across many sites. Search engines reveal some of it, while directories, people-search sites and data brokers hold more. Our guide to personal information exposure explains where to look and what each kind of exposure means.

Why a manual check can miss things

A manual search is a great start, but it will not show everything. Some people-search profiles do not rank in Google, data brokers keep records that are never published, and breach data is not searchable at all. That is why a good check combines your own searches with a breach check and a look at the big people-search sites directly.

You can do a thorough check without paying anything. These free tools cover most of it:

What to do if you find your information

Take a breath. Finding your details online is very common, and most of it can be reduced. Work through your list from high risk to low:

  • People-search profiles: use each site's free opt-out. Our data broker removal guide lists 25 sites with their steps.
  • Google results: request removal through Results about you or Google's removal form.
  • Old accounts: delete the ones you no longer use. See how to find old accounts you forgot about.
  • Breached passwords: change them, starting with your email and bank, and turn on two-step sign-in.

For the full process, follow our guide to removing personal information from the internet. If the list is long, compare data removal services by country to see which one fits.

Can a digital footprint be completely removed?

Not completely. You can remove a great deal of what strangers see, but copies, public records and breach data tend to remain, and new information appears over time. A realistic goal is a smaller, safer footprint that you check regularly. Our guide on how to reduce your digital footprint and keep it smaller shows how.

How often should you check your digital footprint?

Every three months is a sensible rhythm for most people, because that is roughly how often people-search listings reappear. Check straight away after a big life change, such as a move, a new job, a divorce or a new phone number. Our guides on how often to check your digital footprint and auditing it after a life change go into more detail.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the easiest way to check my digital footprint?

Search your name in quotation marks, then your emails, phone numbers and usernames in a private browser window. Add a free breach check for your email and look yourself up on the big people-search sites.

Can I check my digital footprint for free?

Yes. Search engines, reverse image search, Google's Results about you and breach checks such as our free email check cost nothing. Paid services mainly help with removal and ongoing monitoring.

What information should I search for?

Your full name and its variations, every email address and phone number you have used, your usernames, your profile photos and your home address. Those are the details strangers use to find you.

Can someone else see my digital footprint?

Anything public can be seen by anyone, including people-search profiles, public social posts and search results. Content behind privacy settings is visible only to the people you allow.

Does checking my digital footprint remove information?

No. Checking shows you what is out there. Removal is a separate step: opt-outs, removal requests, privacy settings or a data removal service.

How long does a digital footprint check take?

A first check takes about half an hour if you follow the eight steps above. Later checks are quicker because you know where to look.
