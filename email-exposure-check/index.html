Email Exposure Check: Find Where Your Email Is Online

An email address can become a persistent identifier across the internet. Old accounts, forums, social profiles, directories and data broker databases may continue to associate information with an address long after you stop using a website.

TL;DR — How to check your email exposure:

Search your email address in quotation marks, check older email addresses, review public profiles and look for people-search or data broker listings. Finding your email online does not automatically mean your account has been hacked. If you find unnecessary exposure, remove it where possible and review your online presence periodically.

Your email address can connect different pieces of your online identity. When the same address is used across multiple services, it may make it easier to associate accounts, profiles and publicly available information.

An exposed email address can also attract unwanted marketing, phishing attempts and other unsolicited messages. Exposure does not automatically mean that an account has been hacked, but it is worth understanding where your address is publicly visible.

How to Check Where Your Email Appears Online

  1. Search the exact email address.

    Put your email address in quotation marks in a search engine. Review the results carefully and look for profiles, directories, forums and websites that display the address.

  2. Search older email addresses.

    If you have used multiple addresses over the years, check each one. Abandoned addresses can remain associated with old accounts.

  3. Check your social profiles.

    Review whether your email address is visible publicly on social networking profiles or other public accounts.

  4. Review old accounts.

    Think about websites and services you joined in the past. Close accounts you no longer need and update security settings on accounts you keep.

  5. Check people-search and data broker listings.

    Some databases may associate email addresses with names, locations or other information. Review people-search sites and data broker listings for additional exposure.

Email Exposure Does Not Always Mean a Data Breach

Finding your email address online does not by itself prove that your account has been compromised. An address may be publicly visible because you used it to register for a website, publish a profile or communicate on a forum.

A data breach is a separate issue. If you suspect that an account has been compromised, change the password, enable multi-factor authentication where available and follow the affected service's security guidance.

How to Reduce Email Exposure

  • Remove your email from public profiles where it is not necessary.
  • Close unused online accounts.
  • Use privacy settings on social platforms.
  • Consider separate email addresses for different purposes.
  • Request removal from websites that publish your information.
  • Review your online exposure periodically.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I check if my email is exposed online?

Search the exact email address in quotation marks, review public profiles and check websites such as directories, people-search services and data broker listings.

Does finding my email address online mean it was leaked?

No. An email address may be publicly visible because it was published on a profile, website, forum or directory. Public exposure and a data breach are separate issues.

Can I remove my email address from the internet?

You may be able to remove it from individual websites and listings, but there is no single process that removes an email address from every website or database.

Should I use different email addresses?

Using separate addresses for different purposes can help limit how widely one address becomes associated with your online activity.
