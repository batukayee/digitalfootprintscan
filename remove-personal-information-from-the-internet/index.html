How to Remove Personal Information From the Internet

Personal information can appear across search results, data broker profiles, people-search sites, social networks, old accounts and other websites. This guide explains how to find that information and reduce your public exposure step by step.

Removing personal information from the internet is usually a process rather than a single request. Some information can be removed directly from the website that published it. Other information may require an opt-out request, an account deletion, or a search-engine removal request.

Quick answer — how to remove personal information:

First find what is exposed by searching your name, email addresses, phone number and usernames. Then remove information from the original websites where possible, opt out of data broker and people-search listings, manage eligible search results, and delete or secure old accounts. Recheck periodically because information can appear again.

What Personal Information Can Appear Online?

The information connected to you online can come from many different sources. Some of it may have been shared by you, while other information may have been published, collected or combined by third parties.

  • Full names and name variations
  • Email addresses
  • Phone numbers
  • Home or previous addresses
  • Social media profiles
  • Usernames and account names
  • Photos and publicly visible profile information
  • Old forum posts, comments and websites
  • People-search profiles and directory listings
  • Information associated with data broker profiles

Where Your Personal Information May Be Found

Before removing anything, it helps to understand where the information is coming from. Different sources require different removal methods.

Search engines

Search engines can make personal information easier to discover. A search result is not necessarily the original source, however. Removing a result from a search engine and removing the information from the website hosting it are separate actions.

Data brokers

Data brokers may compile information from multiple sources and create profiles associated with individuals. These profiles can contain contact information and other details.

Learn more about data broker removal and how the opt-out process works.

People-search sites

People-search websites can make information about individuals searchable through names, phone numbers, addresses and other identifiers.

See our guide to people-search sites for more information.

Social media and old accounts

Old social profiles, public posts, profile details and unused accounts can continue to expose information long after you stop actively using them.

How to Remove Your Personal Information From the Internet

  1. Find what is exposed.

    Search your full name, name variations, email addresses, phone number and commonly used usernames. Check several search results and look beyond the first page.

    For a broader starting point, use our digital footprint check guide to work through the main areas of online exposure.

  2. Record the websites that contain your information.

    Keep a simple record of the page URL, the type of information exposed, the action you need to take and the date you submitted a request. This makes it easier to track removals and follow up later.

  3. Remove information from the original website.

    When possible, start with the website that hosts the information. Look for privacy, removal, deletion or opt-out instructions. If the information comes from an account you control, remove unnecessary information or delete the account where appropriate.

  4. Opt out of data broker and people-search listings.

    Find the privacy or opt-out process for each relevant service and submit the required request. Some services may require you to confirm your identity or verify the request by email.

    Because this process can involve many different websites, our data broker removal guide explains the process in more detail.

  5. Request eligible search-result removals.

    Search engines may provide removal processes for certain categories of personal information appearing in search results. Remember that removing a result from a search engine does not necessarily remove the information from the original website.

  6. Clean up old social profiles and accounts.

    Review accounts you no longer use. Remove unnecessary contact details, private information and old public posts where possible, and delete accounts you no longer need.

  7. Check again after removal.

    Search again after your requests have been processed. Keep your records and repeat important checks periodically because information can be republished, collected again or appear on another website.

How to Remove Personal Information From Google Search

Google provides processes for requesting removal of certain types of private personal information from Search. These processes apply to eligible search results and do not automatically delete the underlying information from the website that hosts it.

If your address, phone number or email address appears in Google Search, you can investigate Google's available personal-information removal options and determine whether the result qualifies.

The best approach is usually to treat search-result removal and source removal as two separate tasks: reduce visibility in search where appropriate, while also contacting the original website when you want the underlying information removed.

How to Remove Your Information From Data Brokers

Data broker removal generally involves identifying the companies holding information about you and submitting an opt-out or deletion request through their privacy process.

The process can become time-consuming when information appears across multiple services. Some people handle requests individually, while others choose a privacy service to help manage removals.

For a dedicated explanation of the process, see Data Broker Removal.

How to Remove Information From People-Search Sites

People-search sites are another important source to check when reducing online exposure. Search for yourself using your name, phone number and other identifiers, then review the privacy or opt-out instructions for listings you find.

Because different sites use different procedures, keep a record of each request and verify that the listing has actually disappeared.

Our people-search sites guide explains why these sites matter when reviewing your digital footprint.

What About Email Addresses and Phone Numbers?

Email addresses and phone numbers can be particularly useful identifiers when searching for information connected to you.

Search each email address you have used publicly and check where it appears. You can also review your email exposure separately.

For phone numbers, try different formats and check directories and people-search services. Our phone number exposure guide covers this area in more detail.

Can You Completely Remove Yourself From the Internet?

Usually, complete removal is not realistic. Information can exist across many independent websites and databases, and removing it from one source does not guarantee that another copy does not exist elsewhere.

A more practical goal is to reduce unnecessary public exposure, remove information where possible, secure accounts, limit future sharing and periodically check what remains visible.

Why Personal Information Can Reappear

Removing a listing does not necessarily prevent information from being collected again. Public records, websites and other sources can continue to supply information to services that build or update profiles.

This is why privacy cleanup is better viewed as an ongoing process than a one-time project.

How to Reduce Future Online Exposure

Once you have cleaned up existing information, a few habits can help reduce future exposure.

  • Review the privacy settings of important accounts.
  • Delete old accounts you no longer need.
  • Avoid publicly posting unnecessary contact information.
  • Use different usernames where appropriate.
  • Review what personal information appears on public profiles.
  • Periodically search for your own information online.
  • Keep a record of important removal requests.

Should You Remove Personal Information Yourself or Use a Service?

Manual removal can be appropriate when you have a limited number of listings and are comfortable managing the requests yourself. It gives you direct control over which websites you contact and what information you ask them to remove.

A privacy service may be useful when information appears across many sources or when you want ongoing monitoring and removal assistance. Before using one, compare what sources it covers, how often it checks for new listings, what removal process it uses and what the service actually promises.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I completely remove my personal information from the internet?

Usually not completely. You can reduce a significant amount of public exposure by removing information from source websites, opting out of data broker and people-search listings, managing eligible search results, and deleting unnecessary accounts.

How do I remove my personal information from Google?

Google provides removal processes for certain types of personal information appearing in Search. Removing a Google result does not necessarily remove the information from the original website, so source removal may also be necessary.

How do I remove my information from data brokers?

Identify the relevant data broker listing, locate its privacy or opt-out process, submit the required request and verify that the listing has been removed. Some people use privacy services to manage multiple removal requests.

Does deleting social media remove my information from the internet?

Deleting or restricting an account can reduce exposure, but it does not guarantee that copies, screenshots, cached information, third-party references or previously collected data no longer exist.

Why does my personal information come back after I remove it?

Information can be collected again from public records, websites and other sources. Data broker profiles may also be rebuilt or updated, which is why periodic checking can be necessary.
