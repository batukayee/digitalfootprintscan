UK GDPR Erasure Request: How to Ask for Deletion

UK law lets you ask an organization to delete your data. The ICO's guidance explains the deadline, the limits and the order in which to complain.

By Digital Footprint ScanUpdated October 11, 2026
TL;DR

The UK Information Commissioner's Office says you can ask for your data to be deleted by speaking or writing to the organization, with no set wording. It must respond within one calendar month, extendable by up to two more months for complex requests with notice. The request is usually free, the organization may ask for ID, and the right is not absolute. If you are unhappy, complain to the organization first and then to the ICO.

After Brexit, the UK kept its own version of the GDPR, enforced by the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO). The rules on erasure are similar to the EU's, but the practical steps and the regulator are different. Here is how a UK request works.

What the ICO says

The ICO's page for the public explains that you have a right to ask organizations to delete your personal data. The right is sometimes called the right to be forgotten or the right to erasure. You can make the request verbally or in writing. There is no set wording, though the ICO provides a template you can adapt.

The organization should answer within one calendar month. If your request is complex, it can extend the deadline by up to two more months, but it must tell you. The request is usually free. The organization may ask you to prove your identity so that it does not delete the wrong person's data.

The right is not absolute. The ICO notes that organizations may refuse in certain cases, such as freedom of expression, legal obligations, public interest, archiving or research, and legal claims. A refusal should come with an explanation.

Building a clear request

A good request is short and specific. Include:

  • Your full name and any account or reference number
  • The email address or phone number you used
  • What you want erased, for example "my account and all personal data you hold about me"
  • That you are making a request under the UK GDPR
  • The date, so the one-month limit is clear

Send it in a way that leaves a record, such as email or a web form. Written requests are easier to prove. If you phone, follow up in writing.

Steps to take

1Find the contact

Look for a data protection officer or privacy contact in the organization's privacy notice.

2Send the request

Use the ICO template if it helps. Keep a copy and a time stamp.

3Respond to ID checks

If the organization asks for proof of identity, provide the minimum needed.

4Watch the deadline

Mark one calendar month from receipt. If the organization asks for an extension, check that it explained why it was needed.

5Complain if necessary

The ICO advises making a complaint to the organization first. Then, if you remain unhappy, you can complain to the ICO.

Do not treat this as a legal prerequisite. The ICO recommends going to the organization first, but that is advice about process, not a statement that you lose any rights by skipping it.

New complaint handling rules

The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 added obligations for organizations to help people make data protection complaints, for example through an electronic complaint form. According to the ICO, organizations must acknowledge a complaint within 30 days and respond without undue delay. The ICO says all of the Act's data protection provisions are in force.

Where erasure fits among other privacy steps

Asking an organization to delete data is different from asking a search engine to remove a link. See Google's right to be forgotten for search results. If your data was involved in a breach

The ICO recommends that people affected by a breach complain to the organization, keep records, monitor their finances and beware of impersonation scams. Frequently asked questions

Do I have to write to the organization first?

The ICO advises complaining to the organization first, but this is guidance on process, not a stated legal condition.

How long does the organization have?

One calendar month, with an extension of up to two more months for complex requests if it tells you.

Does it cost anything?

The ICO says it is usually free.

Can the organization refuse?

Yes. The right is not absolute, and exceptions include freedom of expression, legal obligations, public interest, archiving, research and legal claims.

Do I need special wording?

No. You can make the request verbally or in writing, and the ICO offers a template.
