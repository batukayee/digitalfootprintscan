CRA and GCKey Accounts Hijacked: What the Report Found

No government database was cracked. Attackers simply logged in, using usernames, passwords and security answers that had leaked from other websites.

By Digital Footprint ScanUpdated October 11, 2026
TL;DR

Canada's privacy commissioner reported to Parliament on February 15, 2024 that attackers used credential stuffing to reach about 26,000 Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) My Accounts and about 6,000 Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) accounts in 2020. The OPC found both departments broke the Privacy Act, and both now require multi-factor authentication. See what a leaked password can unlock.

Government accounts hold some of the most useful details a thief can want: a social insurance number, a date of birth, a bank deposit setting. In 2020, attackers did not need to defeat government security to get at those accounts. They arrived with keys that people had already lost somewhere else.

What happened

According to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, attackers used credential stuffing in July and August 2020. Bots tried usernames, passwords and security-question answers that had been stolen in earlier breaches of other services. When a combination matched, the bot was in.

The CRA detected the attack on August 7, 2020 after a tip from law enforcement. It shut its online portal on August 15, and ESDC shut its own portal on August 20. The OPC published its special report to Parliament on February 15, 2024.

How it worked

Credential stuffing depends on one human habit: reusing logins. If the same email and password protect a shopping site and a tax account, a breach of the shopping site becomes a key to the tax account.

At the CRA the OPC found an additional weakness. A misconfiguration about 20 months old caused the system to skip the security-question check when a login came from a new device. The very control meant to catch unfamiliar logins was not firing.

What was exposed

The OPC reports that attackers used about 26,000 CRA My Accounts and one Represent a Client account, plus about 6,000 ESDC My Service Canada Accounts and 112 ESDC business accounts. In terms of people, it counted about 34,000 affected CRA individuals and about 14,000 affected ESDC individuals.

The exposed information included contact details, identifiers such as social insurance numbers and dates of birth, and financial, banking and employment information. Attackers changed direct deposit and address details and applied for benefits and tax refunds. ESDC found more than 2,000 identity theft cases, and the Represent a Client breach exposed information on more than 8,000 individuals tied to 36 businesses.

How it ended

The OPC found that both departments contravened the Privacy Act, on unauthorized disclosure and on accuracy. Both accepted recommendations. Since the breach, both departments have made multi-factor authentication mandatory and added checks on direct-deposit changes. Shared Services Canada began offering optional multi-factor authentication for GCKey from September 1, 2023.

For individuals the outcome depends on the case. Some had benefit payments redirected or tax returns filed in their name, and repairing that takes time even after the fix.

Key facts at a glance

  • Method: credential stuffing with logins stolen elsewhere
  • Period: July to August 2020
  • CRA My Accounts used: about 26,000
  • ESDC accounts used: about 6,000 personal and 112 business
  • Individuals affected: about 34,000 (CRA) and 14,000 (ESDC)
  • Report date: February 15, 2024

What the report does not say

We have used only the counts and findings the OPC gives. The special report is the source for every figure here, and we have not added details from other coverage. For a similar account takeover story in another country, see the 23andMe case.

Why this case matters

Government portals are attractive because one login can lead to several things at once: a tax record, a benefit payment and a bank deposit setting. That makes a reused password more dangerous here than on a typical shopping site.

The case also shows how a small configuration error can undo a good control. A security question that is skipped on new devices protects nobody. Mandatory multi-factor authentication, which both departments now use, is a much stronger barrier because a leaked password alone no longer opens the door.

Frequently asked questions

What is credential stuffing?

It is an attack where bots try logins stolen from one site on many other sites. It works because many people reuse the same password and security answers.

Were CRA and ESDC systems hacked?

The OPC describes attackers logging in with real credentials, not breaking the databases directly. A CRA misconfiguration also allowed a security question to be skipped on new devices.

How many people were affected?

The OPC counted about 34,000 individuals at the CRA and about 14,000 at ESDC, with more than 2,000 identity theft cases found at ESDC.

Is multi-factor authentication required now?

According to the OPC, both departments have made it mandatory since the breach. For GCKey, optional multi-factor authentication began on September 1, 2023.
