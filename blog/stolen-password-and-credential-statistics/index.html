Stolen Password and Credential Statistics (2026)

How many accounts sit in breach data, how much of it comes from stealer malware and credential-stuffing lists, how often identity attacks are password attacks and what multi-factor authentication and passkeys change.

Key takeaways

  • The pool of stolen credentials is vast: 17.8 billion breached accounts across 1,043 breaches in Have I Been Pwned (HIBP). One person can appear many times, so this is not a count of people.
  • Credential data built for reuse is part of that pool. HIBP lists a 2-billion-record credential-stuffing dataset added in November 2025 and several stealer-log collections of 100 million records or more.
  • Passwords are the main target. More than 97% of identity attacks Microsoft sees are password attacks, and identity-based attacks rose 32% in the first half of 2025 (Microsoft).
  • MFA changes the odds. Microsoft says MFA can block more than 99% of identity-based attacks.
  • Passkeys performed better in one industry index. At nine large companies, passkey sign-ins succeeded 93% of the time against 63% for other methods (FIDO Alliance).

Methodology and limitations

Every figure on this page comes from the organization that published it, such as a regulator, a government agency or the original report. We read the source page or document directly, and we record the period each figure covers. When a number could not be confirmed against a primary source, we left it out. Rows marked “Our calculation” are simple arithmetic on published figures, and the inputs are named in the same row.

  • HIBP counts accounts in the breaches it has loaded. It does not hold all breaches, and the same person can appear many times.
  • Microsoft’s figures describe the identity attacks it observes, over July 2024 to June 2025.
  • The 99% MFA figure is Microsoft’s statement of what MFA can block, not a measured outcome across all accounts.
  • The FIDO figures come from nine large companies and a survey, so they are a best case rather than an average.

Counts of reports, complaints and notifications measure what was reported or notified, not everything that happened. Currency symbols follow the source. Where an agency does not name its currency, we show the symbol the source uses (for example, C$ for Canada and A$ for Australia are our labels). Figures from different countries use different definitions and should not be added together. Spot an error? Tell us and include the source link; we correct verified errors and note the date at the top of the page.

Frequently asked questions

How many passwords have been stolen?

No one has a full count. Have I Been Pwned lists 17.8 billion breached accounts across 1,043 breaches, but accounts are not unique people and many are duplicates across breaches (Have I Been Pwned).

What is credential stuffing?

Attackers take leaked email and password pairs and try them on other sites. HIBP lists a 2-billion-record dataset named Synthient Credential Stuffing Threat Data, added on November 6, 2025 (Have I Been Pwned). The risk depends on whether you reuse passwords.

Does multi-factor authentication stop password theft?

Microsoft says MFA can block more than 99% of identity-based attacks, and that more than 97% of identity attacks are password attacks (Microsoft Digital Defense Report 2025).

Are passkeys better than passwords?

In the FIDO Alliance’s October 2025 index of nine companies, passkey sign-ins succeeded 93% of the time versus 63% for other methods and were faster (8.5 seconds against 31.2 for traditional MFA) (FIDO Alliance Passkey Index).

What should I do if my password was in a breach?

Change that password and any account that reused it, turn on MFA and check the breach details. Our guide to what to do next walks through it.
