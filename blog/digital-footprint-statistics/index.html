Digital Footprint Statistics (2026)

How many people are online, how much of their lives sits on platforms, and how often that data gets exposed. Every figure comes from a regulator, a statistics agency or an original study.

By Digital Footprint ScanUpdated October 12, 202642 statistics · 12 sourcesCite this page
TL;DR

About 6.04 billion people, 73.2% of the world, were online by October 2025, according to DataReportal. Exposure is routine rather than rare: a US tracker counted a record 3,322 data compromises in 2025, and 56% of UK adults told the ICO they had experienced a data breach in the past 12 months or earlier. Key takeaways

  • The audience is almost everyone. DataReportal counts 6.04 billion internet users (73.2% of the world population) in October 2025, and Pew finds 96% of US adults online.
  • The footprint is spread across many services. In the US, 84% of adults use YouTube and 71% use Facebook (Pew, 2025). UK adults spend an average of 4 hours 30 minutes a day online (Ofcom).
  • Exposure is measured in billions of records. Have I Been Pwned lists 1,043 breaches and 17.8 billion breached accounts (HIBP). Accounts are not people, because one person can appear many times.
  • The US recorded 3,322 data compromises in 2025, the highest annual count the Identity Theft Resource Center has reported (ITRC).
  • Breach experience is common in surveys: 56% of UK adults say they have experienced a data breach (ICO), 43% of Canadians say they have been impacted by a privacy breach (OPC) and 38% of Australians were told in the past 12 months that their data was in one (OAIC). The questions differ, so the figures are not directly comparable.

The BJS figure comes from the most recent Identity Theft Supplement it has published, covering 2021. Survey wording differs by country: the ICO asks whether people have experienced a breach in the past 12 months or before, the Australian question asks whether people were told in the last 12 months, and the Canadian question asks whether people have been impacted. Do not rank countries using these percentages.

The ITRC series shows why raw notice counts need care. The number of victim notices fell from about 1.37 billion in 2024 to about 279 million in 2025, while the number of reported compromises rose to a record. A drop in notices reflects which breaches happened to be large in each year; it does not mean fewer people are exposed overall, and it does not show that fewer organizations were breached.

What the numbers mean for an ordinary person

These statistics describe populations, not any one person. They show that exposure is common and that many breach notices leave out basic facts: in 70% of 2025 US breach notices the cause of the incident was not stated (ITRC). Our guides on the digital footprint basics and on how to check your own footprint turn these points into steps.

Methodology and limitations

Every figure on this page comes from the organization that published it, such as a regulator, a government agency or the original report. We read the source page or document directly, and we record the period each figure covers. When a number could not be confirmed against a primary source, we left it out. Rows marked “Our calculation” are simple arithmetic on published figures, and the inputs are named in the same row.

  • DataReportal compiles third-party data, including modelled estimates, and revises prior-year figures. Its totals are best read as estimates.
  • Pew and Ofcom survey different populations and define use differently, so US and UK platform shares are shown separately.
  • Have I Been Pwned counts accounts in the breaches it has loaded. It does not hold every breach, and one person can appear in many records.
  • The BJS identity theft figures cover 2021, which the agency lists as the latest year of data available from its Identity Theft Supplement.

Counts of reports, complaints and notifications measure what was reported or notified, not everything that happened. Currency symbols follow the source. Where an agency does not name its currency, we show the symbol the source uses (for example, C$ for Canada and A$ for Australia are our labels). Figures from different countries use different definitions and should not be added together. Spot an error? Tell us and include the source link; we correct verified errors and note the date at the top of the page.

Frequently asked questions

How many people use the internet?

DataReportal estimated 6.04 billion internet users in October 2025, or 73.2% of a world population of 8.25 billion. The figure includes revised data for India, so the year-on-year increase of 294 million is partly a correction. Source: DataReportal, Digital 2026 Global Overview.

How many Americans use YouTube and Facebook?

In Pew Research Center’s 2025 survey of 5,022 US adults, 84% said they use YouTube and 71% said they use Facebook. Instagram was next at 50%. Source: Pew Research Center, Social Media Fact Sheet.

How many data breaches happen each year?

The Identity Theft Resource Center counted 3,322 US data compromises in 2025, up from 3,152 in 2024. That count covers publicly reported US incidents only. Other counts use different methods, and Have I Been Pwned lists 1,043 breaches in total across all years. Sources: ITRC 2025 Annual Data Breach Report; Have I Been Pwned.

Does a bigger digital footprint mean higher risk?

These sources do not measure risk per person. They show that exposure is common. Reducing what is public and closing accounts you no longer use are sensible steps, and our guide to reducing your digital footprint explains how.

Can I use these statistics in my own work?

Yes. Cite the original publisher for each figure and cite this page as the place where the figures are collected and checked. The citation format is in the box above the sources.
