LLC Registration: Is Your Home Address Public?

Business registries exist so that people can find and serve a company. If your home is the company address, the registry can become a search result.

By Digital Footprint ScanUpdated October 11, 2026
TL;DR

Business registration records are generally open to the public, and they usually include an address for the company or its registered agent. Delaware's filing office, for example, says an entity search shows the registered agent's name and address, and that LLCs there are not required to list members or managers. Using a registered agent or a separate business address keeps a home address out of the record. Rules differ by state, so check yours before filing.

Starting a small business is often a kitchen-table decision, and the paperwork asks for an address. If you type your home address into a state filing, you may be publishing it. The risk is easy to avoid, but only if you know where the address goes before you submit the form.

Why business registries are open

A state business registry has a practical job. Customers, courts, lenders and agencies need to find a company and send it official papers. For that reason most registries offer a public search, and the records they hold are meant to be looked up by anyone.

The U.S. Small Business Administration explains that a registered agent receives official papers on behalf of a company. The agent must be located in the state, and many owners hire a paid service for the role. That arrangement is the main privacy tool in the whole process.

What a typical filing can show

What appears depends on the state and the entity type, but these are the details to check on the form:

  • The company's name and filing date
  • The registered agent's name and street address
  • A principal or mailing address for the business
  • In some states, names of members, managers or officers

The Delaware Division of Corporations says its entity search shows the registered agent's name and address. It also states that LLCs there are not required to list members or managers. Wyoming's Articles of Organization ask for the agent's name and physical Wyoming address and for an organizer, and the form does not ask for members. Those are facts about specific forms, not a promise that every state works that way.

Check the form, not the marketing. Some formation services advertise privacy. The only reliable test is to search your own company on the state registry and see what a stranger sees.

How a home address gets into the record

Most exposures come from three choices:

  • Using your home as the registered agent address
  • Entering your home as the principal or mailing address
  • Filing annual reports that repeat the address

Once an address is in a registry, it can be copied. Data brokers and people-search sites build profiles from many public sources, and a company filing that links your name to a street is a useful input for them. Our guide on why people-search sites have your address covers that pipeline.

Ways to keep your home address out

1Use a registered agent

Hire a commercial registered agent or use a qualified person with a business office. The agent's address, not yours, is then the one shown on the public record for service of process.

2Choose a separate business address

A commercial mailbox, a coworking address or a virtual office can serve as the principal address. Confirm that your state accepts it and that the provider is a real street address, since some filings reject a bare P.O. box.

3Read what each form asks for

Before you submit, list every field that is public. Look for members, managers, organizers and officers, and decide who should be named.

4Search your own company afterwards

Look it up on the state registry and in a search engine the way a stranger would. If something personal appears, plan your next steps while the filing is recent.

5Update old filings

If a prior filing used your home address, ask your filing office how to amend the address. Amending changes the registry going forward, but copies elsewhere can remain.

Sole proprietors and "doing business as" names

You do not need an LLC to create a record. Many places require a trade name or "doing business as" registration, a local business license or a sales tax permit. These filings can list the owner's name and an address. Ask the local office whether the address field is public and whether a business mailing address is accepted.

What registration privacy does not cover

A registered agent does not hide your name if your state requires it, and it does not remove records already copied elsewhere. Banks, tax agencies and licensing boards may still hold your home address in files that are not public. The aim is to keep the public record clean, not to make you untraceable.

Frequently asked questions

Is an LLC's address always public?

Registries are generally open, so assume the filed address is searchable. What is shown varies by state, so check the entity search for your state.

Do I have to list my name on an LLC filing?

It depends on the state and the form. Delaware says LLCs there are not required to list members or managers, but other states may differ.

Does a registered agent protect my privacy?

It keeps your home address off the agent line of the public record because the agent's address is used instead. It does not control what other filings or records show.

Can I change an address I already filed?

Most filing offices have an amendment process. Contact yours for the form and fee. Copies already taken by third parties may remain online.

Will a business address stop people-search sites listing me?

Not by itself. Listings come from many sources, so it helps to also check your data broker profiles.
