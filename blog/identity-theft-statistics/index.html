Identity Theft Statistics (2026)

Complaint counts, losses and the most common types of identity theft in the US, UK, Canada and Australia, taken from government agencies and fraud-prevention bodies.

By Digital Footprint ScanUpdated October 12, 202641 statistics · 12 sourcesCite this page
TL;DR

The FBI received 31,675 identity theft complaints in 2025, up 48% from 21,403 in 2024. A US federal survey found 23.9 million people aged 16+ were victims in a single year, yet only about 7% reported it to police. In the UK, Cifas recorded more than 242,000 identity fraud cases in 2025.

Key takeaways

  • Complaints are rising fast but are a small slice of the problem. FBI identity theft complaints rose 48% in 2025 (our calculation), while the BJS survey estimates 23.9 million US victims in 2021 and says only about 7% reported to police.
  • Credit cards are the most common target. In the FTC’s 2024 data, credit card identity theft accounted for 449,032 of 1,135,291 identity theft reports (FTC).
  • In the UK, identity fraud is more than half of the National Fraud Database: more than 242,000 cases, 54% of all cases filed in 2025 (Cifas).
  • Most people affected by a breach do not report identity theft afterwards. In Australia, 8% of breach-affected adults reported identity theft and 16% reported financial or credit fraud (OAIC, 2026).
  • Reporting systems differ, so compare trends within a source rather than totals across sources.

The United States: complaints and losses

Three US sources count identity theft in different ways. The Federal Trade Commission collects reports from consumers and from other agencies. The FBI’s Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) counts complaints about internet-enabled crime. The Bureau of Justice Statistics (BJS) surveys households, so it captures victims who never report. The totals are not meant to match.

What the BJS survey adds

The BJS Identity Theft Supplement asks a nationally representative sample of people aged 16 and over about their own experience. Its latest data year is 2021. It is the best available guide to how much identity theft goes unreported.

The contrast with the complaint data is large. The FTC logged about 1.1 million identity theft reports in 2024 and the FBI about 21,000, while the BJS estimated 23.9 million victims in 2021. The surveys and the complaint systems count different things, and the years differ, so the figures cannot be subtracted from each other. They do show that most incidents never reach a regulator. The most common form in the BJS survey was misuse of an existing account, not a new account opened in someone’s name.

Methodology and limitations

Every figure on this page comes from the organization that published it, such as a regulator, a government agency or the original report. We read the source page or document directly, and we record the period each figure covers. When a number could not be confirmed against a primary source, we left it out. Rows marked “Our calculation” are simple arithmetic on published figures, and the inputs are named in the same row.

  • Complaint counts depend on who chooses to report and on how each agency classifies a report. The FBI, FTC and Cifas categories overlap but are not equivalent.
  • The BJS survey covers 2021, the latest year the agency lists for its Identity Theft Supplement, and a 12-month recall period.
  • The IC3 figures count complaints received by the FBI, not every incident.
  • Currency amounts are in US dollars.

Counts of reports, complaints and notifications measure what was reported or notified, not everything that happened. Currency symbols follow the source. Where an agency does not name its currency, we show the symbol the source uses (for example, C$ for Canada and A$ for Australia are our labels). Figures from different countries use different definitions and should not be added together. Spot an error? Tell us and include the source link; we correct verified errors and note the date at the top of the page.

Frequently asked questions

How many people are victims of identity theft each year?

The most recent US federal survey estimate is 23.9 million people aged 16+ in 2021, which was 9% of that population (BJS, Victims of Identity Theft, 2021). Complaint systems record far fewer: the FTC received 1,135,291 identity theft reports in 2024 (FTC Consumer Sentinel Data Book 2024).

Is identity theft increasing?

In the FBI’s complaint data, yes: 19,778 complaints in 2023, 21,403 in 2024 and 31,675 in 2025. In the UK, Cifas reported identity fraud cases down 3% in 2025 at more than 242,000, although total fraud cases rose 6% (Cifas Fraudscape 2026).

What is the most common type of identity theft?

In the FTC’s 2024 data, credit card identity theft was the largest named category with 449,032 reports, followed by “other identity theft” at 358,993 (FTC Consumer Sentinel Data Book 2024). In the BJS survey, misuse of an existing credit card was the most common single form, at almost 4% of people (BJS, Victims of Identity Theft, 2021).

How many victims report identity theft to police?

About 7% of victims in the BJS 2021 survey said they reported it to police (BJS, Victims of Identity Theft, 2021).

Does a data breach lead to identity theft?

Not necessarily. In the 2026 OAIC survey of Australians affected by a breach, 8% of Australians whose data had been in a breach reported identity theft and 16% reported financial or credit fraud (OAIC Australian Community Attitudes to Privacy Survey 2026).
