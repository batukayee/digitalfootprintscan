Active vs Passive Digital Footprint: Examples

One kind of footprint comes from what you choose to post. The other comes from what is recorded while you simply go about your day.

By Digital Footprint ScanUpdated October 11, 2026
TL;DR

An active digital footprint is data you deliberately share, such as posts, comments, forms and profiles. A passive digital footprint is data collected about you without an obvious action, such as location data, browsing history, device identifiers and cookies. The distinction matters because you control the first directly and the second mostly through settings, tools and choices about which services to use.

When people say "digital footprint", they usually picture a social media profile. That is only half of it. The other half builds up quietly. Separating the two makes it clear what you can change by deciding and what you can change only by adjusting settings.

The short definition

An active footprint is information you knowingly put out. A passive footprint is information gathered about you as a result of using devices, apps and websites, often without a deliberate action on your part.

If you want the wider definition first, read what is a digital footprint.

Active footprint examples

You create an active footprint when you do something on purpose. Typical examples:

  • Posting a photo, comment, review or video
  • Filling in a sign-up form with your name, email and birth date
  • Creating a public profile on a professional or dating site
  • Signing a petition or entering a competition
  • Sending an email or message, which the recipient can keep or forward
  • Registering a domain, a business or a professional license

The common thread is intent. You knew you were sharing, even if you did not think about who else would see it.

Passive footprint examples

A passive footprint accumulates from activity itself:

  • Browsing history and search queries recorded by sites and browsers
  • Cookies, advertising identifiers and device identifiers
  • Location data from phones, apps and fitness trackers
  • IP address logs kept by websites and networks
  • Purchase history held by stores and payment services
  • Records that other people create, such as a friend tagging you or a county recording a deed

The last item shows why the line is blurry. Information others publish about you is neither active nor fully passive for you, but it still belongs in your footprint.

Passive does not mean harmless. The data is not secret, merely unnoticed. Location, browsing and purchase patterns can describe your habits more closely than a single post can.

A side-by-side view

Active footprint

Created by you on purpose. You can usually edit, delete or make it private.

Passive footprint

Collected as a by-product. You can limit it with settings and tools, but rarely erase it everywhere.

Why the difference matters

With an active footprint, the question is "what should I share?" The control is your own judgment before you post. With a passive footprint, the question is "what is being collected, and who gets it?" The control is in app permissions, browser settings, account dashboards and privacy laws.

Data brokers draw on both. Public records and purchased data feed the passive side, and public profiles feed the active side. That is how a people-search site can assemble a profile you never wrote.

How to reduce each type

1Audit what you share on purpose

Review your public profiles, old posts and forms. Delete or restrict what you no longer need, as described in reduce your digital footprint.

2Check app permissions

Turn off location and contacts access where an app does not need it.

3Review account dashboards

Services like Google let you view and delete saved activity. See what Google knows about you.

4Limit tracking in your browser

Use browser privacy settings and block third-party cookies where practical.

5Find the copies others made

Search your name and check people-search listings. Opt out where you can.

Do not aim for zero

Nobody can have no footprint and still use modern services. A better goal is a footprint that you chose, that you understand and that is as small as your life allows. A scan like ours is a starting point for that.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between active and passive digital footprints?

Active is data you deliberately share. Passive is data collected about you, such as location and browsing history, often without a deliberate action.

Which footprint is bigger for most people?

For many people the passive footprint is larger, since it accumulates automatically. Active footprints are easier to see.

Is a tagged photo active or passive?

It is created by someone else. It is part of your footprint but not an action you took, so treat it as a third category.

Can I delete a passive footprint?

Partly. You can reduce collection and delete saved activity in some accounts, but you rarely erase all copies.

Where do I start?

Search your own name, then check account settings and listings. The digital footprint check is a guided way to do that.
