Online Privacy Attitudes Statistics (2026)

What people in the US, Canada, Australia and the UK say about privacy: how worried they are, how much control they feel they have, whom they trust and how much they understand.

By Siddharth ShankarUpdated October 12, 202642 statistics · 6 sourcesCite this page
TL;DR

Large majorities say they have lost control. In Pew’s US survey, 73% of adults say they have little or no control over data companies collect. In Canada, 89% are at least somewhat concerned about their privacy and 91% about identity theft. In Australia, 91% say organizations that hold data are responsible for protecting it.

Key takeaways

  • Control is the common complaint. 73% of US adults feel they have little or no control over data collected by companies and 79% say the same for government (Pew, 2023).
  • Concern is high and rising in places: 91% of Canadians are at least somewhat concerned about identity theft, with the “extremely concerned” share rising from 47% in 2022 to 54% in 2025 (OPC).
  • Trust in technology companies is low. 12% of Canadians have at least a fair amount of trust in social media companies to protect their information (OPC). 40% agree that businesses respect their privacy rights.
  • Privacy policies do not help much. 71% of Canadians find them difficult to understand, and 69% of US adults see them as something to get past (Pew).
  • Australians increasingly look to regulators: 29% say government or regulators are responsible for protecting personal information, up from 15% in 2023 (OAIC).

Methodology and limitations

Every figure on this page comes from the organization that published it, such as a regulator, a government agency or the original report. We read the source page or document directly, and we record the period each figure covers. When a number could not be confirmed against a primary source, we left it out. Rows marked “Our calculation” are simple arithmetic on published figures, and the inputs are named in the same row.

  • The surveys ask different questions, use different samples and fieldwork dates, and are run by different bodies. Do not rank countries using these percentages.
  • Pew’s survey is from May 2023, the most recent we verified. Attitudes may have changed since.
  • The OPC and OAIC report trend comparisons with earlier surveys. We show the prior value where the source gives it, and wording changes between survey waves may affect comparability.
  • Survey results are self-reported. They measure perception, not actual practice or actual exposure.

Counts of reports, complaints and notifications measure what was reported or notified, not everything that happened. Currency symbols follow the source. Where an agency does not name its currency, we show the symbol the source uses (for example, C$ for Canada and A$ for Australia are our labels). Figures from different countries use different definitions and should not be added together. Spot an error? Tell us and include the source link; we correct verified errors and note the date at the top of the page.

Frequently asked questions

What percentage of people are concerned about online privacy?

In Canada’s 2024–25 survey, 89% were at least somewhat concerned about protecting their privacy and 36% were extremely concerned (OPC Canada survey 2024–25). In the US, 71% were worried about how the government uses data in Pew’s 2023 survey (Pew Research Center, 2023).

Do people feel in control of their personal data?

Mostly not. 73% of US adults say they have little or no control over data collected by companies, and 79% say that about government (Pew Research Center, 2023).

Do people read privacy policies?

Many do not. 56% of US adults say they frequently click agree without reading, and 71% of Canadians find policies difficult to understand (Pew Research Center, 2023; OPC Canada survey 2024–25).

Do people trust tech companies with their data?

In Canada, 28% have at least a fair amount of trust in Big Tech to protect their information and 12% in social media companies. 40% agree that businesses respect their privacy rights (OPC Canada survey 2024–25).

Who do people think is responsible for protecting their data?

In Australia, 91% say the organizations that hold the information are responsible, 56% say third-party providers, 29% say government or regulators and 3% say individuals (OAIC Australian Community Attitudes to Privacy Survey 2026).
