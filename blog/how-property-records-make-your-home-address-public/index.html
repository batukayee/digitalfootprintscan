How Property Records Make Your Home Address Public

Buying or owning a home creates records that counties publish for good reasons. The side effect is that your address and ownership are easy to find.

By Digital Footprint ScanUpdated October 11, 2026
TL;DR

In the United States, county recorders and assessors keep deeds and tax rolls that are open to public inspection. Those records can show ownership, mailing address and assessed values, and property sites and data brokers republish them. You usually cannot remove the county record, but you can limit where else it appears and use address-confidentiality options if eligible.

Few records are as basic as who owns a property. Governments publish them so buyers can check title, neighbors can see tax assessments and lenders can verify claims. The side effect is that an address linked to your name is easy to find, often through a chain of websites that never contact the county.

What counties keep

Two county offices produce most of the records people see:

  • The recorder or clerk-recorder holds deeds and other property documents. One California county clerk-recorder says the law requires that the recorder's records be open to the public, and that documents are kept in a single public repository known as the Official Records.
  • The assessor keeps the assessment roll. Another California county assessor says ownership information is part of the roll and available for public inspection. The roll includes ownership, mailing address and assessed values.

These are California examples, so check the rules of your own county. In general, the structure is similar across the United States, but access, fees and what is shown vary.

What a record can reveal

Depending on the county and the document, a property record can show:

  • The owner's name and the property address
  • A mailing address if it differs
  • Assessed value and tax information
  • Deeds and transfers over time
  • Mortgage documents

A name linked to a physical address is the core privacy exposure. For people at risk, that link matters more than the value of the home.

How it spreads online

Counties are not the only publishers. Real estate sites, data brokers and people-search services copy and combine records. A listing on a people-search site might show your home address because a deed was recorded years ago. See why people-search sites have my address.

Real estate portals also display home facts and estimated values. Our guide on Zillow and Redfin covers what you can edit there.

Removing a copy is not removing the record. A people-search listing or portal page can be removed or edited, but the county record usually remains. Plan to manage the copies, not erase the source.

What you can do

1Find the copies

Search your address and your name. Use how to search for your address and name online as a starting guide.

2Opt out of people-search sites

Follow how to opt out of people-search websites for sites you find.

3Ask the county what is possible

Some offices have processes for people with safety concerns. Ask what is allowed in your county rather than assuming.

4Check address-confidentiality programs

California's Safe at Home program provides a substitute mailing address that agencies accept in place of a residential address. It is available to people such as victims of domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking, human trafficking and child abduction, plus certain health care workers and public employees who fear for their safety. Other states have similar programs.

5Consider how you hold title

Some owners use legal structures. These raise legal and tax questions, so get qualified advice before changing how property is held. For businesses, see LLC and business registration address privacy.

Special protection for some roles

The Daniel Anderl Judicial Security and Privacy Act, enacted in December 2022, stops data brokers reselling federal judges' personally identifiable information and lets judges have information removed from federal government sites. It applies to federal judges and their families, not to the general public.

What to expect

Property records do not disappear, and new transactions create new ones. A reasonable goal is to remove or hide the copies you can control and know where the unavoidable ones sit. Our broader address-removal guide covers the full process.

Frequently asked questions

Are property records public everywhere?

They are open in many places, but rules differ by county and state, including what is shown and what you can request.

Can I remove my name from a deed record?

Usually not. Recorded documents are part of the official record. Ask your county about what options exist.

Does a PO box help?

It may help for the mailing address on a tax roll, but the property address and ownership may still be recorded.

Do real estate sites remove my home if I ask?

Policies vary. Redfin, for example, says it does not remove property pages, while allowing corrections with proof of ownership.

Does an address confidentiality program change property records?

Programs like Safe at Home provide a substitute address for dealings with agencies. They do not necessarily change records held elsewhere, so check the rules.
