LinkedIn Privacy Settings: What Strangers Can See

LinkedIn is built to be found. That is useful for a job search and less useful when a stranger is researching you.

By Digital Footprint ScanUpdated October 11, 2026
TL;DR

A LinkedIn profile is public-facing by design, and the settings that control who can see it live under Settings & Privacy. Recent third-party guides describe a public-profile visibility toggle, profile viewing options such as private mode, and controls over who can see your connections and activity. LinkedIn moves and renames these settings, so look under Settings > Visibility and compare against what you see before changing anything.

LinkedIn is where many people put their full name, employer, city, school and photo in one place. That is the point of the site. It is also why a LinkedIn profile is often the first result when someone searches your name.

Public profile versus logged-in view

Two audiences see your profile differently. People who are signed in to LinkedIn may see more, depending on your settings and your connection to them. People who are not signed in, including anyone using a search engine, see only what your public profile settings allow.

The simplest test is to open your profile link in a private browser window while signed out. What you see is close to what a stranger sees.

LinkedIn moves its settings. We could not verify the current menu names directly in a live account. The labels below come from two independent 2026 guides, so treat them as a map and read what your own screen says.

The settings worth reviewing

Guides published in 2026 describe these areas under Settings & Privacy, in a section commonly called Visibility:

  • Edit your public profile: a toggle for your profile's public visibility. Described as turning it off to remove the profile from search engines.
  • Profile viewing options: modes such as a private profile characteristic or full private mode, which change what other people see when you view their profile.
  • Visibility of your LinkedIn activity: controls over whether updates are shared with your network.
  • Who can see your connections: a control over whether others can browse your connection list.

Email visibility and options for who can find you by email or phone have also moved around between versions. Rather than relying on a name, scan the Visibility area for anything mentioning email, phone or discovery.

What a stranger can learn from a typical profile

Think of the profile as a reference sheet for someone doing research on you:

  • Full name and photo, which can be matched to other sites
  • Current and past employers, helpful for scam messages that sound plausible
  • City or region
  • School and graduation years, which hint at your age
  • Contact details, if you added them

None of this is secret, which is the point. The goal is to choose how much of it is open to people who have no relationship with you. For context, see what strangers can find about you.

How to review your profile

1View your profile signed out

Open it in a private window, or ask a friend to look. Note what shows beyond your name and headline.

2Open Settings & Privacy and find Visibility

Read each option before changing it. If the name differs from what you expected, look for the closest match rather than guessing.

3Decide on public visibility

If you do not need strangers or recruiters to find you through a search engine, consider limiting public profile content. If you are job hunting, you may prefer more visibility. Choose deliberately.

4Trim contact details and dates

Remove a personal phone number, a home address or exact birth date. Keep a work email if you want inbound messages.

5Check your connections and activity settings

Decide whether your connection list and your updates should be visible to everyone in your network.

6Search your name again

After the changes, search for yourself. Search engines can take time to update, so recheck in a few weeks.

Job searching and privacy are a trade-off

A very private profile is not always the right choice. Recruiters use LinkedIn because it makes people findable. If you are searching for work, a visible profile with limited personal details usually works better than a hidden one. Our guide on checking your online presence before a job application walks through the same trade-off from the employer's side.

Impersonation and scam messages

Details on your profile help scammers write a believable message. Be careful with unexpected job offers, requests to move to another app and links that ask you to log in again. If you are unsure, go to LinkedIn directly instead of using the link, as general phishing advice recommends.

Frequently asked questions

Can strangers see my LinkedIn profile without an account?

That depends on your public profile settings. Open your profile signed out to see exactly what a non-member would see.

Does turning off public visibility hide me from Google?

Guides describe the public-visibility setting as removing the profile from search engines, but results already indexed can take time to disappear. Check your own account labels.

Will people know I viewed their profile?

It depends on your profile viewing mode. Review the options in Settings > Visibility.

Should I put my phone number on LinkedIn?

Only if you want to be contacted that way. A personal number can be copied into other listings, so consider a work number or leave it off.

What if my LinkedIn photo shows up elsewhere?

Run a name search and image search. For listings on other sites, see how to find yourself on people-search sites.
