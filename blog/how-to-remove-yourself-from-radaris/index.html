Radaris Opt-Out 2026: Court Order Moves the Domains

A New Jersey court has moved Radaris's domains out of its operators' control. Here is what that changes for anyone looking for a Radaris opt-out.

By Digital Footprint ScanUpdated October 12, 2026
TL;DR

In 2026 a New Jersey court entered a final default judgment against Radaris.com, Rehold.com and Trustoria.com and transferred the Radaris domain to Atlas Data Privacy Corporation. The notice on radaris.com offers no opt-out form, so do not look for one there.

Earlier versions of this guide walked through a Radaris profile suppression. That process is no longer the right place to start. The Radaris website now shows a court notice instead of the people-search service it used to run.

What the court notice says

The notice on radaris.com says the domain was transferred to Atlas Data Privacy Corporation under a final New Jersey court judgment and is no longer controlled by its former operators. The case is Atlas Data Privacy Corporation, et al. v. Radaris.com, et al., Superior Court of New Jersey, Law Division, Middlesex County, Docket No. MID-L-000847-24.

Key dates

  • February 8, 2024: Atlas and five individual plaintiffs filed the lawsuit.
  • May 27, 2025: An amended complaint was filed, asserting claims assigned to Atlas by about 21,760 covered persons under New Jersey's Daniel's Law.
  • August 27, 2026: The court entered a final default judgment against Radaris.com, Rehold.com and Trustoria.com.
  • September 16, 2026: KrebsOnSecurity reported that the judge ordered radaris.com and more than a dozen other data broker domains transferred to the plaintiffs.

What the judgment does

According to the notice, the court found that the plaintiffs established violations of Daniel's Law, ordered the domain transferred to protect the privacy and safety of covered persons, and issued a permanent injunction. The injunction bars disclosing the home addresses and unpublished home telephone numbers of the individual plaintiffs and covered persons through the three named sites, their subdomains and affiliated sites, and related databases or records. Daniel's Law is a New Jersey law aimed at protecting the home addresses and unpublished phone numbers of covered persons such as law enforcement personnel.

What it means if you are looking for a Radaris opt-out

The Radaris notice page does not describe an opt-out process or request form. It points readers to the Superior Court of New Jersey for case information and lists support@atlas.net as the contact address for Atlas Data Privacy Corporation. We do not know what will happen to the data behind the old Radaris profiles, so do not assume a profile has been erased.

Be careful with lookalike pages. Because many people search for a Radaris removal, unrelated sites may claim to remove your Radaris profile. Use only the official court notice and the contact it lists, and do not pay a third party to submit a form that no longer exists.

What to do instead

The same kinds of details usually sit on many sites at once, so the court order does not clean up your exposure. Check the other major sites with how to find yourself on people-search sites and then work through How to Remove Yourself From People-Search Sites. Our guides for Spokeo, Whitepages and BeenVerified cover three of the largest.

If you are a covered person

If you may qualify as a covered person under Daniel's Law, the notice and the court case are the relevant sources. We are not lawyers, so read the notice and ask the court or a lawyer about eligibility.

Checking for old copies

Old Radaris results can remain in search engines for a while. See why removed information can still appear in search results and how to verify a removal for how to check, and what to do when removed information keeps reappearing if details turn up on a new site.

Frequently asked questions

Can I still opt out of Radaris?

The radaris.com page now shows a court notice and describes no opt-out process or request form.

Who controls radaris.com now?

The notice says it was transferred to Atlas Data Privacy Corporation under a final New Jersey court judgment.

Does this mean my data was deleted?

No statement on the notice says that. Check other sites that may hold the same details.

Which other domains were named?

The judgment names Radaris.com, Rehold.com and Trustoria.com.
