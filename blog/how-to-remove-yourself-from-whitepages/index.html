How to Remove Yourself From Whitepages (2026 Opt-Out Guide)

Whitepages is one of the largest people-search sites, and most listings come from public records rather than anything you signed up for. Here is exactly how to get yours suppressed.

By Digital Footprint ScanUpdated September 14, 2026
TL;DR

Find your listing on whitepages.com, copy its exact profile URL, submit it through the official suppression-request form, choose a reason, and verify by phone. Most listings are suppressed within 24–48 hours. Whitepages continuously re-ingests public records, so the same listing can reappear later and is worth rechecking periodically.

If you have ever searched your own name, there is a good chance a Whitepages listing showed up near the top — usually with your age range, current and past addresses, phone numbers, and a list of relatives. Whitepages did not ask your permission to publish any of it. Like most people-search sites, it built that profile by combining public records you have no direct control over.

The good news is that Whitepages does offer a free suppression process, and it is faster than most competitors. The steps below walk through it exactly as it currently works, plus what to expect afterward.

What a Whitepages profile typically shows

Before opting out, it helps to know what you are actually removing. A standard Whitepages listing can include:

  • Full name and known aliases or name variations
  • An age range (not an exact birthdate)
  • Current and previous home addresses
  • Current and previous phone numbers, sometimes marked landline or mobile
  • Names of relatives and known associates
  • Links to a separate, more detailed Whitepages Premium background report
Standard listing vs. Premium report: the free public listing is what shows up in a basic search. Whitepages Premium is a separate, more detailed background-report product. Suppressing your standard listing is the process below; if a Premium report persists afterward, it may need to be addressed separately through Whitepages’ support channel.

Where Whitepages gets this information

Whitepages does not collect data directly from you. It aggregates from public records — property and tax records, court and voter records, and other publicly filed documents — along with data shared by other data brokers and marketing databases. That is why a listing can exist even if you have never used Whitepages or any related service, and why old addresses and past phone numbers often linger long after they are current.

How to remove yourself from Whitepages

1Find your exact listing

Go to whitepages.com and search your name, adding your city and state to narrow the results if multiple people share your name. Open the listing and double-check the details — age range, past addresses, listed relatives — to confirm it is actually you before removing it.

2Copy the exact profile URL

Copy the full URL from your browser’s address bar while viewing your specific listing. Whitepages uses this URL, not just your name, to identify which record to suppress.

3Go to the official suppression-request page

Navigate to whitepages.com/suppression-requests — this is Whitepages’ own opt-out form, separate from any third-party or paid removal page. Paste the listing URL you copied and continue.

4Select a reason for removal

Choose an option from the dropdown menu, such as wanting to keep the information private. Then continue to the next step.

5Verify by phone

Whitepages verifies identity through an automated phone call rather than email. Enter your phone number, answer the call, and enter the verification code it provides when prompted.

6Submit and wait

Once verified, the request is submitted. Removal typically completes within 24–48 hours, though it can occasionally take longer. If you have more than one listing — common if you have lived at several addresses — repeat the process separately for each one, since there is no bulk-removal option.

How to confirm it worked

Wait at least a few hours after phone verification, then search your name on whitepages.com again. A successfully suppressed listing should no longer appear, and the specific URL you submitted should show a removed-record message instead of a profile page. Search engines may keep showing a cached version of the old listing for a short while after removal even once Whitepages itself no longer displays it.

This is not a one-time fix. Whitepages continuously re-ingests public records and data from other brokers, so a removed listing can reappear later, sometimes with slightly different details. Treat this as something to recheck periodically rather than a single task to cross off.

What if the form does not work?

If the automated tool errors out or the URL will not submit, Whitepages also offers a support-ticket option for the same request. It takes longer to process than the automated tool but does not require phone verification, which some people prefer.

Does removing Whitepages remove me from other sites too?

No. Whitepages is one of dozens of people-search sites and data brokers, and each one runs its own separate database and opt-out process. Removing a listing here has no effect on Spokeo, BeenVerified, Intelius, or any other site — each needs its own suppression request. That repetition is exactly the problem paid removal services are built to solve, since they submit and track these requests across hundreds of sites on an ongoing basis rather than as a single one-time cleanup.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Whitepages opt-out actually free?

Yes. The standard suppression-request process does not require payment. Paid services exist, but they are for convenience — handling many sites at once — not because Whitepages itself charges for removal.

How long does Whitepages removal take?

Most listings are suppressed within 24–48 hours after phone verification, though it can occasionally take longer.

Why did my Whitepages listing come back after I removed it?

Whitepages regularly refreshes its database from public records and other data sources. A new or updated public record can generate a new listing even after a previous one was successfully suppressed.

Do I need to opt out of Whitepages Premium separately?

The Premium background-report product is separate from the free standard listing. If a Premium report remains visible after suppressing your standard listing, it may need to be addressed through Whitepages’ support channel directly.

Can I remove a listing for a relative instead of myself?

Whitepages’ suppression process is intended for removing your own listing. Requests involving someone else’s information typically require that person’s own involvement or a different process.

Will removing my Whitepages listing stop spam calls?

It can help reduce one source of exposure, but phone numbers are often listed on multiple sites simultaneously, so Whitepages removal alone is unlikely to stop all unwanted calls.
