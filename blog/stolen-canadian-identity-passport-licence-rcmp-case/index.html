Stolen Canadian Identity: Passport, Licence, Health Card

Identity theft is often described as emptying a bank account. This case shows the other kind: using someone's name to live a whole second life, with real government documents.

By Digital Footprint ScanUpdated October 11, 2026
TL;DR

An RCMP release dated February 11, 2026 says a man was found guilty of nine offences after assuming the identity of a living Canadian citizen in 2012. He was sentenced to one year of incarceration followed by probation and barred from entering Canada for three years. Using the stolen identity, he obtained a citizenship certificate, passport, driver's licence and health card. See what identity theft looks like.

A stolen identity is usually discovered by the person it was taken from, when a bill arrives or a credit application is refused. In this case the discovery came from a border check, and it led back to an identity that had been assumed in 2012.

What happened

The RCMP reported that a man was found guilty of nine offences under the Citizenship Act, the Criminal Code, the Customs Act and the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act. The offences included identity fraud and the use of a forged passport. His sentence was one year of incarceration followed by probation, and he was barred from entering Canada for three years.

According to the release, he obtained the personal information of a living Canadian citizen and assumed that identity in 2012. He then used it to build a further false identity. The release was published on February 11, 2026. The guilty finding is described as having been made on December 17, but the release does not state the year.

How it worked

Using the borrowed identity, he obtained a fraudulent citizenship certificate, passport, driver's licence and health card. Each document helped to get the next one. A driver's licence can support an application for a passport, and a passport can support access to services, which is why a single stolen identity can grow into a whole set of papers.

The case came to light when Canada Border Services Agency officers arrested him as he tried to re-enter Canada under a false identity. That arrest started the Cornwall RCMP investigation. The release adds that he was wanted in New Jersey for failing to appear.

What was exposed

A real citizen's personal information was used to obtain government identity documents. The release does not say what that information was, how it was originally obtained or whether the person it belonged to suffered losses. We have not filled those gaps.

What the case does show is that the core identifiers in a person's life, such as name, birth date and place of birth, can be enough to start a chain of applications if no step along the way catches the mismatch.

How it ended

This is a conviction that ends a long-running case, not a recovery. The sentence is one year of incarceration plus probation and a three-year ban from Canada. Documents obtained through fraud typically need to be cancelled and reissued, and the person whose identity was used can face months of checks to prove who they are, but the release does not describe any of that.

Key facts at a glance

  • Identity assumed: 2012
  • Charges: nine offences, including identity fraud and forged passport
  • Documents obtained: citizenship certificate, passport, driver's licence, health card
  • Sentence: one year of incarceration, then probation, and a three-year ban from Canada
  • How it surfaced: arrest at a border crossing while using a false identity
  • Release date: February 11, 2026

Frequently asked questions

What is identity fraud?

It is using another person's personal information, without permission, to obtain documents, credit or services. In this case it included a passport and a driver's licence.

What was the sentence?

One year of incarceration followed by probation, and a ban from entering Canada for three years, according to the RCMP release of February 11, 2026.

How was the fraud discovered?

Border officers arrested him while he was trying to re-enter Canada under a false identity, which led to the RCMP investigation.

How can I tell if my identity is being misused?

Check your credit reports, government accounts and mail for items you did not create, and act quickly with a fraud alert or freeze if you find any.
