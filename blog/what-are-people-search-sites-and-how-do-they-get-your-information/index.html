What Are People-Search Sites and How Do They Get Your Information?

A practical, privacy-first guide to what are people-search sites and how do they get your information?, with clear next steps and realistic limits.

TL;DR

Find and verify the exact profile. Review the information shown. Use the site’s current opt-out process. Then identify the source, choose the appropriate privacy or removal action, and recheck important results later.

What Are People-Search Sites and How Do They Get Your Information? is usually a source-and-profile problem. People-search services can combine information from several places, so finding the exact profile and understanding its source is more useful than treating every result as a mystery.

Find the exact profile

Search for your name with enough context to distinguish you from people with similar names. Confirm the profile before submitting a request.

Understand where the profile came from

People-search services can compile information from public records, public profiles and other data providers. You may never have had a direct relationship with the service.

If you want the broader context, the People Search Sites guide is the natural next step from this narrower question.

Use the current opt-out process

Follow the instructions published by the service itself. Avoid relying on old third-party screenshots or outdated steps.

Keep a record

Save the profile URL, submission date and confirmation. This turns a one-off request into a trackable task.

Source you control

Edit, hide or delete the information at the source when appropriate.

Third-party source

Use the site’s current correction, privacy, opt-out or removal process.

Review the wider ecosystem

One opt-out generally affects one service. If the same information appears elsewhere, handle those sources separately.

Frequently asked questions

Are people-search sites data brokers?

The FTC describes people-search sites as a type of data broker.

Why do they have information about me?

They can compile information from public records, public profiles and other data providers.

Does one opt-out remove me everywhere?

No. An opt-out normally applies to the service and profile involved.

Can profiles contain mistakes?

Yes. Compiled data can be outdated or mismatched.

What should I do after an opt-out?

Keep the confirmation and recheck the profile later, then review other related services.
