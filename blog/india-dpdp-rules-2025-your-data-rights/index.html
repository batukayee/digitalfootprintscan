India’s DPDP Rules 2025: Your Data Rights Explained

What the notified Digital Personal Data Protection Rules mean for people in India.

By Siddharth ShankarUpdated October 26, 2026
TL;DR

India notified the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 on 14 November 2025, according to the Press Information Bureau. They give individuals rights to consent, access, correct, update, erase and nominate. Data fiduciaries must address requests within a maximum of 90 days. Compliance is phased over 18 months. Children’s data needs verifiable parental consent, with exceptions, and a digital Data Protection Board will hear complaints, with appeals to TDSAT. Penalties under the Act reach up to ₹250 crore.

India’s data protection framework has taken shape in stages. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 set out the principles, and the Rules fill in the detail. This guide follows the Press Information Bureau’s (PIB) release on the notified Rules and the penalties laid out in the Act. It does not cover points the release leaves out.

What was notified

The PIB reports that the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 were notified on 14 November 2025. The Rules put the Act into operation in phases.

Your rights

The PIB lists these rights for individuals (data principals):

  • to give or withdraw consent;
  • to access information about their personal data;
  • to correct and update it;
  • to erase it;
  • to nominate another person to exercise their rights.

Response times

Data fiduciaries, the organisations that decide how and why personal data is processed, must address requests within a maximum of 90 days.

Phased compliance

The Rules give organisations 18 months to comply in phases. The PIB release does not say when the 18-month period starts for each obligation, so check the official text for dates relevant to a particular service.

Consent managers

The Rules provide for consent managers, who help individuals give, manage, review and withdraw consent through a single interface.

Children’s data

Processing the data of children requires verifiable consent from a parent or guardian, with exceptions set out in the Rules. See the parents’ guide to children’s digital footprints for practical steps.

The Data Protection Board

The Rules set up a digital Data Protection Board with four members. Individuals can complain to the Board, and appeals go to the Telecom Disputes Settlement and Appellate Tribunal (TDSAT). The PIB release does not give a portal address or the deadline for notifying the Board, so we do not state them.

Penalties

Under the Act, penalties can reach:

  • up to ₹250 crore for failing to maintain reasonable security safeguards;
  • up to ₹200 crore for failing to notify a breach, or for breaches of obligations involving children;
  • up to ₹50 crore for other violations.

How to use your rights

  1. Find the privacy notice of the service and see how to make a request.
  2. Write clearly, naming the right you are using (access, correction, erasure or nomination).
  3. Keep a record of the date, the method and the reply.
  4. Allow for the 90-day maximum, but note some requests may be answered sooner.
  5. Escalate to the Board if the response is unsatisfactory, once the process is available.
  6. Protect your identifiers. Read how to check Aadhaar authentication history and how to lock Aadhaar biometrics.

Related protections in India

Other tools help with fraud and spam: Sanchar Saathi, TRAI’s Do Not Disturb and the 1930 helpline. For a comparison with Canada’s law, see PIPEDA. To understand what counts as personal data, see what PII is.

Key point. The DPDP Rules give you rights to access, correct, erase and nominate, with a 90-day maximum response time. Compliance is phased over 18 months, so check dates for each service.

A sample request you can adapt

Look for a privacy contact, grievance officer or data request form in the service's privacy notice, and send a short written request. Use the email address or phone number registered with the service so it can find your records.

Sample request under the DPDP Act

Subject: Request as a data principal under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023

I am writing as a data principal. I request that you [give me information about the personal data you process about me] / [correct and update the following: ...] / [erase my personal data] / [record [name] as my nominee to exercise my rights].

Account or contact details you hold: [registered email, phone number, customer ID].

Please acknowledge this request and respond within the period set by the DPDP Rules, 2025.

Before you escalate

  • Use the service's own grievance route first. Keep the acknowledgment, the date and any reference number.
  • Allow the response time. The PIB says data fiduciaries must address requests within a maximum of 90 days.
  • Then go to the Board. Complaints can be taken to the Data Protection Board, with appeals to TDSAT, once its processes are in place for your issue.

Sources

  • Press Information Bureau: Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 notified (November 17, 2025) (rights, timelines, Board, consent managers)

Frequently asked questions

When were the DPDP Rules notified?

On 14 November 2025, according to the Press Information Bureau.

What rights do individuals have?

Consent, access, correction, updating, erasure and nomination.

How long do organisations have to respond?

A maximum of 90 days.

Who hears complaints?

A digital Data Protection Board, with appeals to TDSAT.

What are the maximum penalties?

Under the Act, up to ₹250 crore for failing to keep reasonable security safeguards, ₹200 crore for some breach and child-related failures and ₹50 crore for others.

How do I write a data erasure request in India?

Send a short written request to the service's grievance or privacy contact, say you are a data principal under the DPDP Act, name the right you are using and keep a record of the date.
