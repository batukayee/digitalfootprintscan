Scam Loss Statistics: US, UK, Canada and Australia (2026)

What the main national bodies report about money lost to scams in 2025: totals, the costliest scam types, which platforms they start on and how much goes unreported.

By Digital Footprint ScanUpdated October 12, 202672 statistics · 13 sourcesCite this page
TL;DR

Reported scam and fraud losses in 2025 were $15.9 billion to the FTC and $20.9 billion in internet-crime complaints to the FBI in the US, £1.28 billion in all UK fraud (UK Finance, including unauthorised fraud), A$2.18 billion in Australia (ACCC) and C$704 million in Canada (CAFC). Investment scams are the largest named loss category in each source. Canada’s Competition Bureau says only 5% to 10% of frauds are reported.

Key takeaways

  • US reported losses were $15.9 billion to the FTC in 2025, about 27% above the $12.5 billion of 2024 (our calculation), on about 3 million fraud reports (FTC).
  • The FBI’s separate count is higher: $20.877 billion from 1,008,597 complaints, with investment fraud alone at $8.65 billion (IC3).
  • Investment scams are the largest named loss category in each source, at about 38% to about 50% of reported losses (our calculation, with caveats on page).
  • Scams that start on social media cost US consumers $2.1 billion in 2025, about eight times the 2020 figure (FTC).
  • The true totals are higher than reported. Canada’s Competition Bureau says only 5% to 10% of frauds are reported (Competition Bureau).

Where US scams start: social media

The FTC’s April 2026 spotlight on social media shows both the scale and who is exposed. Nearly 30% of people who reported losing money said the scam started on social media. Facebook had the most reported losses, followed by WhatsApp and then Instagram.

Methodology and limitations

Every figure on this page comes from the organization that published it, such as a regulator, a government agency or the original report. We read the source page or document directly, and we record the period each figure covers. When a number could not be confirmed against a primary source, we left it out. Rows marked “Our calculation” are simple arithmetic on published figures, and the inputs are named in the same row.

  • Loss figures are what victims and institutions reported. The Competition Bureau says only 5% to 10% of frauds are reported in Canada, and the true totals are higher.
  • The FBI and FTC figures use different definitions and categories, so they must not be added together.
  • UK Finance covers fraud through its members’ payment systems; Cifas counts cases filed by its members. Neither is a count of all UK fraud.
  • The ACCC’s national totals and its Scamwatch-only figures are different measures; the 26.5% figure for people aged 65+ refers to Scamwatch losses.
  • The FTC 2025 total comes from a March 2026 testimony release that rounds to about 3 million reports and $15.9 billion. We did not use a 2025 Data Book because we could not retrieve one.

Counts of reports, complaints and notifications measure what was reported or notified, not everything that happened. Currency symbols follow the source. Where an agency does not name its currency, we show the symbol the source uses (for example, C$ for Canada and A$ for Australia are our labels). Figures from different countries use different definitions and should not be added together. Spot an error? Tell us and include the source link; we correct verified errors and note the date at the top of the page.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do people lose to scams each year?

In 2025, reported losses were $15.9 billion to the FTC and $20.877 billion in FBI IC3 internet-crime complaints in the US, £1,279.8 million in all UK fraud (including unauthorised fraud), A$2.18 billion in Australia and more than C$704 million in Canada. Each is a different measurement, so they are not additive (FTC testimony release, Mar 2026; FBI IC3, 2025 report; UK Finance Annual Fraud Report 2026; ACCC Targeting Scams 2025; CAFC, Top frauds of 2025).

What type of scam causes the most losses?

Investment scams. They were $7.9 billion of FTC-reported losses in 2025, $8.65 billion of IC3 losses, £221.5 million of UK APP losses, A$837.7 million in Australia and C$351 million in Canada.

Do scammers target older people more?

The data shows large losses among older people, but reporting differs by country. IC3 recorded $7.748 billion in complaints from people aged 60+ in 2025. In Australia, people aged 65+ made 26.5% of Scamwatch losses while being 17.1% of the population (FBI IC3, 2025 report; ACCC Targeting Scams 2025).

How many scams go unreported?

No one knows exactly. Canada’s Competition Bureau says only 5% to 10% of frauds are reported (Competition Bureau Canada, Fraud Prevention Month 2026). The US Bureau of Justice Statistics found that about 7% of identity theft victims reported to police (BJS, Victims of Identity Theft, 2021), covered on our identity theft statistics page.

Where do scams start?

In the FTC’s 2025 data, nearly 30% of people who lost money said the scam started on social media. In the UK, UK Finance data shows 66% of APP cases enabled by online sources and 17% by telecommunications, though it says they cannot be considered definitive (FTC social media data spotlight, Apr 2026; UK Finance Annual Fraud Report 2026).
