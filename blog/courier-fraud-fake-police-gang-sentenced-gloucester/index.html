Courier Fraud: Fake Police Calls and a Taxi to the Bank

The caller said they were police and the cash was evidence. A taxi was waiting. Victims were told what to say to the bank, and a courier arrived to collect the money.

By Digital Footprint ScanUpdated October 11, 2026
TL;DR

The Crown Prosecution Service says four men were sentenced at Gloucester Crown Court on June 10, 2022 to a combined 21 years for courier fraud. They made more than 7,500 calls from offices in London and defrauded £430,452 by posing as police investigating counterfeit currency. See how scammers get your number.

Courier fraud relies on a telephone call and a person at the door. The call convinces the victim that something is wrong with their bank account. The courier turns the victim's trust into cash. This case shows how the pieces were coordinated.

What happened

The CPS reported that four men were sentenced at Gloucester Crown Court on June 10, 2022 to a combined 21 years: 6 years; 4 years and 8 months; 5 years and 4 months; and 4 years and 8 months. They had pleaded guilty on August 6 and November 10, 2021.

The gang operated from offices in London and made more than 7,500 calls to victims across the country. They defrauded £430,452. Gloucestershire Police and the CPS Specialist Fraud Division brought the case.

How it worked

They posed as police officers investigating counterfeit currency involving the victim's bank accounts. Victims were told to withdraw money and hand it to a courier, supposedly as part of the investigation. Accomplices played the parts of senior police officers or bank officials.

The gang arranged taxis to take victims to their banks and coached them on what to say to bank staff. That coaching was designed to get around the usual question a bank teller asks when a customer takes out a large sum.

What was exposed

Victims' cash savings and pension income were taken. The release does not describe any data breach or how the gang selected victims. The key exposure was a person's willingness to trust a caller who sounded official and who could add apparent authority by transferring the call to another "officer."

How it ended

The case ended with convictions and prison terms. A combined 21 years is a significant outcome. The CPS release does not say whether any of the £430,452 was recovered.

How the sentences were split

The four sentences differed: 6 years; 4 years and 8 months; 5 years and 4 months; and 4 years and 8 months. The release does not explain what each man did, so we do not assign roles to individuals. The pleas came in two stages, on August 6 and November 10, 2021, and sentencing followed in June 2022.

What the total shows is that investigators treated the operation as a coordinated enterprise, with offices, a call schedule and couriers, not as a series of separate frauds.

Key facts at a glance

  • Combined sentence: 21 years across four men
  • Sentencing date: June 10, 2022
  • Calls made: more than 7,500
  • Amount defrauded: £430,452
  • Method: fake police, a "courier" collecting cash, taxis and coaching
  • Court: Gloucester Crown Court

How to protect yourself and your family

1Remember that police and banks never ask you to withdraw cash for them

They will not send a courier for your money either.

2End the call and use a different phone

Wait at least 15 minutes if you can, and call 101 or your bank on a number you look up.

3Ignore coaching about what to tell the bank

Anyone who tells you to mislead your bank is a scammer.

4Share the rule with older relatives

What the release does not say

The CPS release does not say how the gang found victims or how much money was recovered. For a case where couriers also collected bank cards, see the San Diego bank impersonation scam.

Why this case matters

Courier fraud gets people to take part in their own loss, which makes it harder to catch than a hacked account. The victim withdraws the money, believes the explanation and may hold back from telling family because the caller asked for secrecy.

The use of taxis and scripted answers for bank staff shows how much preparation the gang put in. A bank teller's question is meant to protect a customer. If you ever find yourself being coached on how to answer it, treat that as the strongest sign of a scam.

Frequently asked questions

What is courier fraud?

A scam where callers pose as police or bank staff, convince a victim to withdraw cash or hand over cards, and send a courier to collect them.

How many people were sentenced?

Four men, to a combined 21 years, on June 10, 2022 at Gloucester Crown Court.

How much was taken?

£430,452, according to the CPS, from victims across the country.

What should I do if I get such a call?

Hang up, wait or use a different phone, then call 101 or your bank on a number you look up yourself.
