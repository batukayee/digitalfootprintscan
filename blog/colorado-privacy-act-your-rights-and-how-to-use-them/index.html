Colorado Privacy Act: Your Rights and How to Use Them

Colorado residents can ask companies to access, correct and delete their data and to stop selling it.

By Siddharth ShankarUpdated October 26, 2026
TL;DR

The Colorado Privacy Act took effect on July 1, 2023. It applies to businesses that process personal data of more than 100,000 consumers a year, or sell data of 25,000 or more. Residents can ask to know, access, correct, delete and download their data, and opt out of its sale, targeted advertising and certain profiling. Companies must respond within 45 days, with one possible 45-day extension. Only the attorney general and district attorneys can enforce it, and you can complain through the attorney general’s office.

Colorado is one of several US states with a general privacy law. This guide uses the state attorney general’s summary page.

When it applies

The Colorado Privacy Act, or CPA, was signed on July 7, 2021 and went into effect on July 1, 2023. It covers entities that do business in Colorado or offer products or services aimed at Colorado residents and that either:

  • process personal data of more than 100,000 consumers in a calendar year, or
  • earn revenue or discounts from selling personal data of 25,000 or more consumers.

Service providers and contractors that handle data for these companies are also covered. Some entities and data are excluded, including financial institutions under the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act and certain data governed by laws such as HIPAA and the Fair Credit Reporting Act.

Your rights

Colorado consumers have the right to:

  • know whether a controller is collecting their personal data,
  • access their personal data,
  • correct their personal data,
  • delete their personal data,
  • download and transfer their data in a portable format,
  • opt out of the sale of personal data, targeted advertising and certain profiling.

Response times

Controllers must respond to requests within 45 days, with one possible 45-day extension.

Universal opt-out

Under the CPA, controllers had to accept opt-out requests through universal opt-out mechanisms from July 1, 2024. The Colorado Department of Law publishes a list of recognised mechanisms. A browser signal is one example of the idea. See how to turn on Global Privacy Control.

Sensitive data

Controllers need consent before collecting or processing sensitive data. That includes data about children under 13, race or ethnicity, religious beliefs, health conditions, sexual orientation, citizenship status and biometric data used for identification. The AG’s page says consent must be affirmative, freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous.

Who enforces it

The Attorney General’s Office and district attorneys have sole enforcement power. The page says private citizens cannot file lawsuits under the CPA. Where a violation can be remedied, the AG or DA had to send a letter giving 60 days to cure, and the page says that cure-notice process applied until January 1, 2025.

Putting your rights to use

Start with the company’s privacy page or request form, say which right you want to use and note the date you sent it. If 45 days pass without an answer, or the company refuses, you can take the matter to the attorney general’s office.

How to file a complaint

The page links to a complaint form at coag.gov/file-complaint and lists (720) 508-6000 for questions and comments.

Limits

The law has exemptions, and it covers only businesses that meet its thresholds. For listings on people-search sites, use each site’s own opt-out page. This is general information, not legal advice.

A sample request you can adapt

You do not need a lawyer or special wording. A short, clear email or web-form message works. Send it from the email address the company knows you by, so it can match you to its records.

Sample Colorado Privacy Act request

Subject: Colorado Privacy Act request

I am a Colorado resident. Under the Colorado Privacy Act, I ask you to [confirm whether you process my personal data and give me a copy] / [correct the following data: ...] / [delete the personal data you hold about me] / [stop selling my personal data and using it for targeted advertising and profiling].

Details you may hold about me: [full name, email addresses, phone numbers, postal address].

Please confirm you have received this request and respond within 45 days. If you refuse any part of it, please explain why and tell me how to appeal.

Tips that make requests work

  • One right per line. Name exactly what you want, such as deletion and an opt-out of sale, so nothing is missed.
  • Expect identity checks. A company may ask you to confirm who you are before it acts. Share only what it needs to match you, and be wary of requests for more ID than the company already holds.
  • Keep a log. Note the date sent, the company, the method and any reference number. The 45-day clock is what you will point to if you need to complain.
  • Use the opt-out signal as well. A universal opt-out such as Global Privacy Control covers sites you visit, but a direct request is still the clearest route for deletion.

Frequently asked questions

When did the Colorado Privacy Act take effect?

July 1, 2023.

Who does it apply to?

Businesses that process personal data of more than 100,000 consumers a year, or sell data of 25,000 or more consumers, and operate in or target Colorado.

What rights do I have?

Know, access, correct, delete and download your data, and opt out of sales, targeted advertising and certain profiling.

How long do companies have to respond?

45 days, with one possible 45-day extension.

Can I sue under the CPA?

No. The AG’s page says only the attorney general and district attorneys can enforce it.

Do I need special wording for a Colorado privacy request?

No. State that you are a Colorado resident, name the right you are using, list the details the company may hold and ask for a response within 45 days.
