How to Remove Yourself From PeopleLooker (2026 Opt-Out Guide)

PeopleLooker offers an online opt-out route for suppressing people-search listings. Here is how to use it and verify the result.

By Digital Footprint Scan · Updated September 14, 2026

TL;DR

Open PeopleLooker’s opt-out page, locate the matching record, provide the requested email, complete verification, submit, and recheck after processing.

👁 Run a free digital footprint scan to see what else is exposed →

PeopleLooker can expose names, addresses, phone numbers, relatives, associates and other public-record information. Listings can be refreshed as source data changes.

How to remove yourself from PeopleLooker

1Open the opt-out page

Use the PeopleLooker opt-out link from its site footer/privacy area or the current suppression route.

2Search for your listing

Use the current opt-out search form to locate the profile that matches you.

3Select the matching record

Review the available information carefully before choosing the record to suppress.

4Provide your email

Enter an email address you control if the form asks for one and complete any verification step.

5Submit the request

Confirm the opt-out request and keep the confirmation for your records.

6Check again

Re-search PeopleLooker after processing. If your listing remains or returns, submit the current opt-out request again or contact support.

Important: Suppression affects PeopleLooker’s listing. It does not remove the same information from other sites.

How to verify removal

Search the same name and location again after processing. Keep any confirmation message and compare the result with your original profile.

Why information can return

People-search databases refresh from public records and other sources, so a later data update can create another listing.

Frequently asked questions

Is PeopleLooker opt-out free?

The self-service opt-out route is intended to be free.

Do I need a subscription?

No purchase should be necessary simply to submit a privacy opt-out.

Can my listing return?

Yes. Refreshed source data can produce a new record.
