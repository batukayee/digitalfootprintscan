What People Can Find About You Before a First Date

A name, a few photos and a city are often enough to start a search. It helps to know what that search shows before someone else runs it.

By Digital Footprint ScanUpdated October 11, 2026
TL;DR

A first date usually starts with a name, a photo and a username, and each of those can be searched. Common results include social profiles, reverse image matches, people-search listings with an age and past addresses, and public records such as court filings. Searching yourself first shows what a match could see. Remove or limit what you would not want a stranger to know, and keep your home address and workplace details off dating profiles.

Meeting someone from an app or an introduction often begins with a quick search. You may be doing the same. The useful question is not whether the search happens, but what it finds when it does.

The starting points

A person looking you up usually has only a few facts:

  • Your first name, or full name if you shared it
  • A photo or a few photos from your profile
  • A username or handle
  • A city or workplace mentioned in a profile

Each of these is a search key. A full name finds social profiles and news. A photo can be matched to other places it appears. A username can lead to accounts on other sites, because many people reuse handles.

What typically turns up

What appears depends on your name, your habits and where you live. Common results include:

  • Social media profiles: public photos, tagged posts and comments.
  • Professional pages: employer, job title and school.
  • People-search listings: an age range, past addresses, relatives and phone numbers. See what information people-search sites show.
  • Public records: property ownership, court filings and professional licenses, depending on your state.
  • News and local items: sports, events and community pages.

Not everything found is accurate. Mixed-up identities are common with common names, and listings can be outdated.

Looking at you versus looking for you. Most people want reassurance, not detail. The aim of your review is to make sure the first screen of results says what you want it to say.

How to search yourself

1Use a private window

Search your full name, your name with your city, and your username. Look at images, not only web results.

2Search your photos

Use a reverse image search on the photos you use on dating or social profiles to see where else they appear.

3Check people-search sites

Look up your name on a few of the main sites and note any listing with your age or address. How to find yourself on people-search sites walks through it.

4Look at public records

Search for court filings and property records in your area. The guide on court records and your name explains what you may find.

5Tighten your accounts

Make personal accounts private, and think about what your public handle and photo show.

What to keep off a dating profile

CISA advises leaving out your birthdate and location on public profiles, turning off geo-tags and controlling tagging, which is good practice for dating as well. Beyond that, think about:

  • Your workplace or neighborhood name
  • Photos that show a house number, school or license plate
  • A username used on other personal accounts
  • Your full legal name until you have met someone and feel comfortable

If you want to move to a messaging app before meeting, consider which one shows your phone number. See WhatsApp, Telegram and Signal: who can see your number.

When a search turns up something wrong

If a listing is outdated or mixes you up with someone else, you can ask the site to remove it. The people-search opt-out guide explains how. For content that is accurate but private, such as an old address, removal gives you more control, though copies can remain elsewhere.

Safety also means not oversharing

Meeting a stranger carries ordinary caution: meet in public, tell a friend where you are and keep your address private. This guide focuses on what a search can find, not on dating safety as a whole.

Frequently asked questions

Will a date really search my name?

Many people do, though it varies. A quick check of your own results is easy and gives you control.

What do people-search sites show about me?

They can show age, past addresses, relatives and phone numbers, depending on the site and the source data.

Can someone find me from a photo?

Reverse image search can show other places a photo appears. Use different photos on dating and public profiles if that is a concern.

Should I remove my listings before dating?

It is reasonable if the listings show your address or phone number. Removal is not instant, so start before you need it.

Is it okay to look someone else up?

Many people do a light check on public information. Stay within public sources and treat what you find as unverified.
