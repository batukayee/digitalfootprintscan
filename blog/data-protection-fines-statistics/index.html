Data Protection Fines Statistics (2026)

How much regulators fine companies for privacy and security failures, which cases set the totals and how fines compare with the number of people affected.

By Digital Footprint ScanUpdated October 12, 202632 statistics · 10 sourcesCite this page
TL;DR

European GDPR fines totaled about €1.2 billion in 2025 and €7.1 billion since 2018, according to DLA Piper. One decision, the Irish regulator’s €530 million fine on TikTok, accounts for almost all of Ireland’s 2025 fines. In the UK, the ICO fined Reddit £14.47 million and Capita £14 million.

Key takeaways

  • GDPR fines were about €1.2 billion in 2025, closely matching 2024, and €7.1 billion cumulatively to January 10, 2026 (DLA Piper).
  • A few decisions dominate. Ireland’s fines in 2025 were just over €530.77 million, and the TikTok decision alone was €530 million, about 99.9% of that total (our calculation) (DPC).
  • Breach notifications keep climbing: DLA Piper counted 443 a day across Europe, up from 363, an increase of 22%.
  • UK fines in the cases we verified ranged from £1.2 million (LastPass UK) to £14.47 million (Reddit), with Capita at £14 million.
  • Fines are small per affected person. Capita’s works out to about £2.12 for each of 6.6 million people (our calculation). Australia’s first Privacy Act penalty, A$5.8 million, is about A$26 for each of 223,000+ people.

Methodology and limitations

Every figure on this page comes from the organization that published it, such as a regulator, a government agency or the original report. We read the source page or document directly, and we record the period each figure covers. When a number could not be confirmed against a primary source, we left it out. Rows marked “Our calculation” are simple arithmetic on published figures, and the inputs are named in the same row.

  • Fines depend on each regulator’s powers and on legal appeals. Some of the penalties above are under appeal or subject to later change.
  • DLA Piper’s totals are its own compilation of publicly reported fines and are not a complete count.
  • We did not find a primary-source total for ICO penalties since 2023, so we do not cite one. Third-party analyses exist but are single-source and are excluded.
  • Per-person figures divide a fine by the number of people the regulator says were affected. They illustrate the scale, not the harm, and use “up to” or “more than” counts, so the true figures vary.

Counts of reports, complaints and notifications measure what was reported or notified, not everything that happened. Currency symbols follow the source. Where an agency does not name its currency, we show the symbol the source uses (for example, C$ for Canada and A$ for Australia are our labels). Figures from different countries use different definitions and should not be added together. Spot an error? Tell us and include the source link; we correct verified errors and note the date at the top of the page.

Frequently asked questions

How much have companies been fined under GDPR?

DLA Piper reports about €1.2 billion in fines in 2025 and €7.1 billion cumulatively to January 10, 2026. The largest fine ever was €1.2 billion against Meta in 2023 (DLA Piper GDPR Fines and Data Breach Survey, Jan 2026).

What was the biggest data protection fine in 2025?

The Irish DPC’s €530 million fine on TikTok, announced May 2, 2025, made up of €485 million for transfers of EEA user data to China and €45 million for a transparency failure (Irish DPC: TikTok decision).

How much did the ICO fine Capita?

£14 million in total, announced October 15, 2025: £8 million on Capita plc and £6 million on Capita Pension Solutions Ltd, for a March 2023 attack affecting 6.6 million people (ICO: Capita fine).

Do fines compensate the people affected?

The sources here describe regulator penalties and do not describe compensation to individuals. Your own rights are covered in our guides on GDPR rights and UK GDPR rights.

Has Australia fined anyone under the Privacy Act?

Yes. In October 2025 Australian Clinical Labs was ordered to pay A$5.8 million in civil penalties, described by the OAIC as the first under the Privacy Act (OAIC: Australian Clinical Labs penalty).
