What Is the Difference Between Removing Information From Google and Removing It From the Internet?

A practical, privacy-first guide to what is the difference between removing information from google and removing it from the internet?, with clear next steps and realistic limits.

By Digital Footprint ScanUpdated September 9, 2026
TL;DR

Identify the source page. Determine whether you control it. Use source removal before Search removal when appropriate. Then identify the source, choose the appropriate privacy or removal action, and recheck important results later.

What Is the Difference Between Removing Information From Google and Removing It From the Internet? is best approached as a sequence of source-level decisions. Identify who controls the information first, then use source removal, Search removal, correction or opt-out processes as appropriate.

Identify who controls the page

The first question is whether you can edit the source. If you can, source removal is usually the most direct path.

Separate source removal from Search removal

Google Search can affect visibility in Search, but it does not normally delete the source website. Treat the two as separate layers.

If you want the broader context, the personal-information removal guide is the natural next step from this narrower question.

Use the process that matches the source

Social profiles, people-search listings, data-broker profiles and ordinary websites can each have different removal routes.

Document the request

Save URLs, dates and confirmations so you can follow up without starting over.

Source you control

Edit, hide or delete the information at the source when appropriate.

Third-party source

Use the site’s current correction, privacy, opt-out or removal process.

Recheck the result

If the source changes but Search remains outdated, an appropriate refresh process may help. If the source still contains the information, return to source-level removal.

Frequently asked questions

Does Search removal delete the website?

No. Search removal affects visibility in the search engine; the source page is separate.

Should I contact the source owner?

If the source still contains the information, that is often the most direct removal path.

What if the source already changed?

If Search still shows an outdated version, an appropriate outdated-content refresh process may help.

Can every piece of information be removed?

No. Some sources are outside your control, some records have legitimate retention, and copies may exist elsewhere.

How do I keep track?

Record URLs, dates, request status and confirmation details.
